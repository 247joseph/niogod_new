§9 and §10 AÜG Explained for CTOs: What Happens After Reclassification

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Engineering leaders usually hear about the AÜG as a compliance topic owned by legal or procurement. The consequences, however, land in engineering: on team members who may suddenly be employees of your company, on budgets, and on the works council agenda. This briefing walks through what happens if an engagement with an external team is reclassified as unlicensed or undisclosed Arbeitnehmerüberlassung, with a focus on §9 and §10 AÜG, and closes with the controls that sit in a CTO's area of responsibility.

The scale of the issue

Arbeitnehmerüberlassung is not an exotic construct in Germany. According to the Bundesagentur für Arbeit, around 622,000 temporary agency workers were in employment subject to social insurance on 30 June 2025, about 1.8 percent of all such employment, spread across 39,000 lending businesses. Each of those relationships runs under a licence and a defined set of rules. The risk discussed here arises when an engagement functions like labour leasing without being set up as such.

Enforcement is active. In its annual report for 2025, German customs stated that the Finanzkontrolle Schwarzarbeit checked 25,800 employers, opened around 98,200 criminal and more than 52,100 administrative offence proceedings across all sectors, and put the damage caused by undeclared work at about EUR 675 million. On the social security side, the Deutsche Rentenversicherung carried out 23,052 status determination procedures in 2024, according to the federal government's answer to a parliamentary question. For a CTO, the practical point is that classification questions are examined routinely, not only after a tip-off.

The starting point

Arbeitnehmerüberlassung requires a licence of the supplying company (§1 Abs. 1 Satz 1 AÜG). The contract must expressly designate the arrangement as Arbeitnehmerüberlassung (§1 Abs. 1 Satz 5 AÜG), and the individual workers must be named with reference to that contract before they start (§1 Abs. 1 Satz 6 AÜG). An arrangement that is labelled Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag but is lived as Überlassung, because client managers direct and integrate the engineers, typically fails both requirements. For vendors operating from outside the EU and EEA, there is an additional obstacle: under §3 Abs. 2 AÜG, a licence for activity from such establishments must be refused.

The consequence chain

1. Contracts become void (§9 Abs. 1 AÜG)

Under §9 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 AÜG, contracts between supplier and client and between supplier and worker are void if the supplier lacks the required licence. Under §9 Abs. 1 Nr. 1a AÜG, the same applies to the worker's contract if the Überlassung was not expressly designated and the worker was not named as required. Nr. 1b covers exceeding the maximum duration of 18 months.

2. Fictitious employment with the client (§10 Abs. 1 AÜG)

Where the worker's contract is void under §9, §10 Abs. 1 AÜG deems an employment relationship between the client and the worker to exist, starting at the time agreed for the start of the work. In practical terms: an engineer you considered an external resource may be your employee by operation of law, with the content of that relationship partly determined by the statute and by the conditions in your organisation.

3. The worker's option to stay (Festhaltenserklärung)

The worker can prevent the fiction by declaring in writing, within one month, that they wish to remain with the supplier. §9 Abs. 2 AÜG sets strict formal requirements: the declaration must be presented in person at an Agentur für Arbeit, dated and identity-checked there, and reach the supplier or client no later than the third day after presentation. A declaration made before the one-month period starts is void, and a repeated declaration is also void (§9 Abs. 3 AÜG). The choice lies with the worker, not with the companies.

4. Administrative fines (§16 AÜG)

A client that lets workers from an unlicensed supplier work for it commits an administrative offence under §16 Abs. 1 Nr. 1a AÜG. Failing to designate the Überlassung or to name the worker is covered by §16 Abs. 1 Nr. 1c and 1d AÜG. Under §16 Abs. 2 AÜG, these offences can be fined up to EUR 30,000 each. The economic benefit from the violation can be recovered in addition.

5. Social security contributions

Where German social security law applies, the deemed employer is responsible for contributions. Where the supplier nonetheless pays the wages, German law provides for joint liability of supplier and client for social security contributions. Withholding contributions can additionally be relevant under §266a StGB. Whether German social security law applies at all depends on where the work is performed, which matters for teams abroad.

6. Works council rights

In companies with a works council, the deployment of temporary workers is subject to participation under §99 BetrVG, as clarified in §14 Abs. 3 AÜG. A team that was never presented to the works council because it was considered an external Werkvertrag can create a co-determination conflict once it is reclassified.

The remote dimension

For teams working entirely from India, the decisive question is whether the AÜG applies at all, which depends on the domestic link of the arrangement. The administrative view of the Bundesagentur für Arbeit has changed on this point, courts are not bound by it, and settled case law is missing. Our separate briefing on the BA's Fachliche Weisungen covers this in detail. For a CTO the practical conclusion is simple: do not rely on territoriality as the only line of defence. On-site visits, relocations and mixed onshore and offshore setups strengthen the link to Germany, and the cleanest protection is an operating model that is not Überlassung in the first place.

Controls in the CTO's area

  1. Work with external teams through a named vendor lead, not through direct tasking of individuals.
  2. Keep external engineers out of internal line management, performance reviews and leave approvals.
  3. Define deliverables and acceptance per sprint or milestone.
  4. Plan on-site visits as occasional, purpose-bound events organised by the vendor.
  5. Review the operating model quarterly with legal or procurement, especially after reorganisations.
  6. Inform the works council early where its rights might be affected, rather than after a dispute.

This article is general information and does not constitute legal advice for your specific situation.
