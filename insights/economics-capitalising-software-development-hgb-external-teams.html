Capitalising Software Development under HGB When External Teams Build It

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When a company pays an external team to build software it will use for years, the CFO has to decide whether that spend is an expense in the current year or an asset on the balance sheet. The answer affects reported profit, equity and, for a scaleup, the numbers investors and lenders look at. Under German commercial and tax law, the contract model with the external team is one of the factors that decides the treatment. The short version: who bears the production risk matters, and that should be considered when the contract is drafted, not when the annual accounts are closed.

Why this question is growing

German companies spend heavily on development, and an increasing share of it is external. According to the Stifterverband's FuE facts for 2024, internal R&D expenditure of the German business sector reached EUR 92.5 billion, up 2.3 percent, while external R&D expenditure grew 3.7 percent to almost EUR 33 billion, now slightly more than a third of total business R&D spending. Business R&D stood at 2.14 percent of GDP. Software is a large part of this. Bitkom expects the German software market to grow 9.9 percent to EUR 58.1 billion in 2026. With more development bought from outside, the accounting treatment of that spend becomes a material question.

The HGB framework

  • §248 Abs. 2 HGB. Self-created intangible assets of fixed assets may be capitalised. This is an option (Aktivierungswahlrecht), not an obligation. Certain items such as brands and customer lists are excluded.
  • §255 Abs. 2a HGB. Only development costs may be capitalised, not research costs. If research and development cannot be reliably separated, capitalisation is not permitted.
  • §268 Abs. 8 HGB. Where self-created intangibles are capitalised, an equivalent amount (net of deferred taxes) is blocked from distribution.
  • Acquired intangibles. Software purchased from a third party must be capitalised as an asset if it is intended for long-term use, subject to the usual exceptions for low-value items.

Tax treatment differs

For the tax balance sheet, §5 Abs. 2 EStG allows intangible assets of fixed assets to be capitalised only if they were acquired for consideration. Self-created intangibles must be expensed for tax purposes. The result is a gap between commercial and tax accounts that leads to deferred taxes when the HGB option is used.

A further point is the BMF letter of 22 February 2022, which allows a one-year useful life for computer hardware and software for data input and processing, as an option from assessment year 2021. Whether and how it applies to individually developed or very large software systems is discussed in the literature and should be checked with a tax adviser before relying on it.

Acquisition or production: the role of the contract

When an external team builds software, the key question is whether the company has acquired an asset or produced one itself with outside help. The prevailing view in German accounting practice looks at who bears the production risk (Herstellungsrisiko).

This is not a mechanical rule. The assessment depends on the overall contract, the degree of control the buyer exercises and how results are specified. But the direction is clear enough that finance should be involved when the contract model is chosen. A contract titled Werkvertrag but run day to day like time and material helps little: what counts is how it is actually performed, not the heading.

Practical steps for CTO and CFO

  1. Agree the intended treatment upfront. Decide whether the company wants to capitalise development costs under HGB, and whether a tax-effective capitalisation is relevant. Then choose the contract design accordingly.
  2. Separate research from development. Structure work packages and sprint goals so that exploratory work can be distinguished from development of a defined product. Without that separation, §255 Abs. 2a HGB blocks capitalisation.
  3. Document acceptance. Abnahme protocols per sprint or milestone are the evidence that a defined result was delivered. They support both the contract classification and the asset valuation.
  4. Record cost by project. Invoices from an external team should be allocable to specific products or modules. A monthly fee with sprint reports per product makes this straightforward.
  5. Plan the useful life and impairment test. A capitalised asset is amortised and must be reviewed for impairment. Product roadmaps should support the useful life assumption.

Scaleups reporting under IFRS

Companies that prepare IFRS accounts for investors apply IAS 38. Development costs must be capitalised there once the criteria for technical feasibility, intention, ability to use or sell, probable future benefits, resources and reliable measurement are met. The logic of documented phases and acceptance is the same, and the contract with the external team should provide the evidence.

The classification of software development as acquisition or production, and the use of accounting options, depend on the specific facts. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice; please involve your auditor and tax adviser.
