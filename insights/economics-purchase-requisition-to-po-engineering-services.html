From Requisition to Purchase Order: Buying Engineering Services Fast

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

In many DACH companies, the slowest part of adding external engineering capacity is not finding a provider. It is the internal path from the moment a CTO decides to buy to the moment a purchase order exists. Requisitions bounce between budget owners, Einkauf asks for documents the engineering team never prepared, legal reviews a contract template that does not fit services, and finance discovers at invoice stage that the tax treatment was never clarified. The result is weeks or months of delay on capacity that was needed yesterday. This guide sets out a procurement sequence for engineering services that works for both scaleups and Mittelstand organisations, and shows where time is usually lost.

Why engineering services are hard to buy

Procurement processes in DACH companies were mostly built for materials and standard services. Engineering capacity is neither. The output is not fully specifiable in advance, the price depends on the contract model, and the legal structure determines whether the arrangement is a service at all or a form of personnel leasing. Einkauf teams know this. In the BME benchmark Top-Kennzahlen im Einkauf 2026, published in November 2026, three quarters of companies showed deficits in procurement transparency, and only 4 percent of chief procurement officers sit on the executive board. Engineering purchases are therefore often handled by a function with limited mandate and limited visibility into technical requirements. The same benchmark found that 93 percent of companies see high or very high potential for AI in the analysis and evaluation of offers, which will make structured, comparable vendor submissions more important, not less.

Demand, meanwhile, is rising. Lünendonk reported in June 2026 that 37 percent of CIOs plan to increase spending on external IT service providers, and Bitkom forecast in July 2026 that the German IT services market will grow by 3.1 percent in 2026. More companies are going through this process, and many are doing it for the first time with a provider outside Germany.

Where time is lost, and how to recover it

Step 1: a vague need

A requisition that says only "external developers, six months" invites every follow-up question in the chain. A one-page scope with the objective, the first two or three work packages, acceptance criteria and the intended contract model answers most of them in advance. It also determines whether the purchase is a Werkvertrag with defined results or a Dienstvertrag for a qualified service, which changes the contract template that legal will use.

Step 4: approval thresholds

Many companies route any spend above a fixed amount to the managing director or the board. An annual engineering engagement easily crosses that line. Rather than approving the full annual value at once, consider approving a defined first phase with a clear decision point. Approval thresholds are respected, and the business sees results before committing the full amount.

Step 6: supplier onboarding

For a provider outside the EU, onboarding includes questions that domestic vendors never raise: tax residency, invoicing in EUR, bank details for international transfer, data transfer mechanisms under DSGVO Art. 44 ff. with Standardvertragsklauseln, and an Art. 28 data processing agreement. A provider that delivers a complete vendor pack on day one saves weeks. Ask for it before selection, not after.

Step 7: the wrong template

Purchasing contract templates written for goods or for temporary staffing do not fit engineering services. A goods template lacks a Leistungsbeschreibung with acceptance criteria. A staffing template implies the client directs individual people, which is the hallmark of Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 AÜG. Legal should start from a service template that keeps direction with the provider and defines Abnahme under §640 BGB for Werkvertrag deliverables.

Step 10: invoice surprises

An invoice from a foreign service provider to a German business is typically subject to the reverse charge procedure under §13b UStG, meaning the recipient accounts for the VAT. Accounts payable needs to know this before the first invoice arrives, along with the purchase order number the provider must quote. Without a PO reference, many invoice workflows stop automatically.

A realistic timeline

With the scope prepared and the vendor pack complete, steps 3 to 8 can typically be completed in two to four weeks in a scaleup and four to eight weeks in a Mittelstand organisation with formal approval boards. These are illustrative ranges based on the number of approval layers, not measured benchmarks. Without preparation, the same steps routinely take a quarter.

A checklist for the engineering lead

  • Write the one-page scope before opening the requisition.
  • Confirm the cost centre and the accounting treatment with controlling.
  • Ask the shortlisted providers for their vendor pack in the first conversation.
  • Agree the contract model with legal before selection, so offers are comparable.
  • Request a phased approval if the annual value crosses your board threshold.
  • Brief accounts payable on reverse charge and PO references before the first invoice.

This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Approval rules, contract templates and tax treatment depend on your organisation and should be confirmed with the relevant functions.
