Forschungszulage 2026 and External Engineering Teams: What Qualifies

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Germany's research allowance, the Forschungszulage, has become considerably more generous in 2026, and many engineering-heavy companies now include it in their budget planning. For anyone working with external engineering teams, one rule deserves attention before contracts are signed: contract research is only eligible if the contractor has its seat in the EU or the EEA. Work commissioned from a vendor in India does not qualify as Auftragsforschung. The answer is not to avoid external teams, but to plan which work streams carry the allowance and which do not, and to structure the engineering portfolio accordingly.

What changed for 2026

According to Forvis Mazars, the Investitionssofortprogramm expanded the allowance for expenses incurred after 31 December 2025:

  • The maximum assessment base rises to EUR 12 million per year.
  • The standard rate is 25 percent, giving a maximum allowance of EUR 3 million.
  • Small and medium-sized enterprises under the EU definition receive 35 percent, up to EUR 4.2 million.
  • Own work by entrepreneurs is credited at EUR 100 per hour, up to 40 hours per week.
  • For R&D projects starting after 31 December 2025, a flat 20 percent supplement for overhead and operating costs applies, without individual proof.

The allowance has two steps. First, the Bescheinigungsstelle Forschungszulage (BSFZ) confirms that a project qualifies as research and development. Then the company applies to its tax office for the allowance on eligible costs.

The rule for contract research

Auftragsforschung is eligible, but under specific conditions, as summarised by REB Steuerberatung with reference to the statute:

  • Only the client (Auftraggeber) may claim the allowance for commissioned work, not the contractor (§2 Abs. 4 FZulG).
  • For contracts concluded after 27 March 2024, 70 percent of the fee paid is eligible; for older contracts, 60 percent (§3 Abs. 4 FZulG).
  • The contractor must have its place of management in an EU member state or an EEA state (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein). If not, none of the contract cost qualifies (§2 Abs. 5 FZulG).

An Indian engineering vendor therefore cannot generate eligible contract research costs, regardless of the quality of the work or the R&D character of the project.

The allowance is a real factor in a cost comparison for genuine R&D. It should be included in the total cost view, alongside rates, coordination and quality, when deciding where such work is done.

What this means for planning

Most engineering work is not research and development in the sense of the Frascati definitions that the BSFZ applies. Integration, maintenance, test automation, migration, tooling, documentation and the implementation of known methods typically do not qualify. Genuinely new technical problems with uncertain outcome do. That distinction gives a practical planning approach.

  1. Map the roadmap. Mark which work streams contain qualifying R&D and which are engineering execution.
  2. Keep qualifying R&D with eligible cost carriers. Wages of your own employees working on certified projects remain eligible. Contract research with EU/EEA partners is eligible at 70 percent.
  3. Use external capacity where the allowance does not apply anyway. Engineering execution around the R&D core is not eligible regardless of where it is done, so location does not change the allowance there.
  4. Free internal researchers for certified work. If your in-house engineers spend part of their time on non-qualifying execution, moving that execution to an external team can increase the share of internal hours on certified projects. Those hours are eligible.
  5. Document the split. The BSFZ and the tax office will want to see which costs belong to the certified project. Clear work package boundaries and time records make that straightforward.

Two frequent misunderstandings

The allowance is not a discount on the vendor's fee. It is set by the tax office and credited against the company's income or corporate tax; an excess is paid out. A loss-making scaleup can therefore still benefit, but only after the assessment, which matters for cash planning.

Subcontracting chains need a closer look. If an EU contractor passes parts of the work to a subcontractor outside the EU or EEA, do not assume the full fee remains eligible. Clarify with your adviser how the chain is assessed before relying on it in a budget.

Context: how much R&D is bought externally

External R&D is not a niche. The Stifterverband reports that external R&D expenditure of German business reached almost EUR 33 billion in 2024, up 3.7 percent and slightly more than a third of total business R&D spending. With that scale, the location rule for Auftragsforschung is a planning factor for many companies, not an edge case.

Questions to clarify with your tax adviser

  • Which projects are already certified by the BSFZ, and which are planned?
  • Does your company meet the EU SME definition, including linked and partner enterprises?
  • Which current external contracts are Auftragsforschung, and where are the contractors' seats?
  • How are internal hours on certified projects recorded?

The eligibility of projects and costs depends on certification by the BSFZ and on the individual case, and details of the Forschungszulage have changed several times in recent years. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice.
