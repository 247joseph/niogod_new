Lohnnebenkosten 2027: New Contribution Ceilings and the Cost per Engineer

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

For German employers, social security cost on engineering salaries rises again on 1 January 2027, and not because contribution rates change. The increase comes from higher Beitragsbemessungsgrenzen, the income ceilings up to which contributions are charged. Engineers typically earn close to or above these ceilings, so they are exactly the employees for whom the change bites. This briefing summarises the 2027 values, quantifies the effect per engineer with an illustrative calculation, and sets out what finance teams should already factor in for 2028.

The 2027 values

The Federal Cabinet adopted the regulation on social security reference values for 2027 in October 2026. As reported by the Deutsche Rentenversicherung, the key figures are as follows. The regulation still required approval by the Bundesrat at the time of the announcement.

Reference value20262027
Contribution ceiling, pension and unemployment insurance (monthly)8,450 EUR8,850 EUR
Contribution ceiling, health and long-term care insurance (monthly)5,812.50 EUR6,375 EUR
Compulsory insurance threshold, health (annual)77,400 EUR84,150 EUR

The health insurance ceiling rises by 562.50 EUR per month, almost 10 percent, which is unusually steep. It combines the regular wage-indexed adjustment with an additional one-off step. On rates, the Federal Ministry of Health set the average additional contribution rate for statutory health insurance at 2.9 percent for 2027, unchanged from 2026, in its announcement of 30 October 2026. Individual insurers can and do charge more or less. The pension contribution rate stays at 18.6 percent in 2027, according to the Rentenversicherungsbericht 2026 adopted by the Cabinet on 18 November 2026.

What it means per engineer

The following is an illustrative calculation of the employer share of the four main contributions. Assumptions: employer pays half of 18.6 percent pension, half of 2.6 percent unemployment, half of 14.6 percent general health plus half of the 2.9 percent average additional rate, and 1.8 percent long-term care. Accident insurance, the U1, U2 and insolvency levies and company pension schemes are excluded, as are regional and individual deviations.

Gross annual salaryEmployer share 2026Employer share 2027Increase
90,000 EUR16,899 EUR17,611 EUR712 EUR
110,000 EUR18,107 EUR19,328 EUR1,221 EUR

At 90,000 EUR, the salary sits below the pension ceiling in both years, so only the health and care ceiling drives the increase. At 110,000 EUR, both ceilings move and the increase almost doubles. For a team of 15 engineers in this salary band, the illustrative additional employer cost is in the range of 11,000 to 18,000 EUR per year before a single salary has been raised.

The 2028 step

The more significant change comes one year later. The Rentenversicherungsbericht 2026 projects a pension contribution rate of 19.8 percent for 2028, up from 18.6 percent, and 21.0 percent by 2040. These are model calculations under current law, not adopted rates. If the 2028 projection holds, the employer share rises by 0.6 percentage points. On a 90,000 EUR salary that is a further 540 EUR per year, before any increase in the 2028 ceiling, which is also likely.

The threshold effect on private health insurance

The compulsory insurance threshold of 84,150 EUR matters for recruiting. Engineers above it can opt for private health insurance, and the employer then pays a capped subsidy instead of a percentage contribution. As the threshold rises, fewer mid-level engineers qualify, which affects both the candidate value proposition and the employer cost mix. Finance teams that model engineering cost with a single average rate miss this shift.

How to budget for it

  1. Model per salary band, not with a flat percentage. The effective employer rate falls as salaries pass each ceiling. A flat 20 or 21 percent on-cost assumption overstates cost for top earners and understates it for the middle.
  2. Update ceilings every January. They are adjusted annually and the direction has been consistently upward.
  3. Add the 2028 pension scenario now. Multi-year plans prepared in late 2026 should carry the 19.8 percent projection as a base case or explicit sensitivity.
  4. Keep salary increases separate. Contribution changes stack on top of merit and market adjustments, which WTW expects at a median of 3.2 percent in Germany for 2027.
  5. Compare against non-payroll capacity. External services invoiced as Sachkosten carry no employer social security. The comparison must still include the provider margin and the legal structure that keeps the arrangement a genuine service.

Questions for your payroll provider

Before the January payroll run, a short review with your payroll provider or tax adviser prevents surprises in the first quarter. Five questions cover most of the risk.

  • Which engineers cross the new health and care ceiling of 6,375 EUR per month, and which remain below it?
  • Which engineers are currently privately insured, and does any of them fall back below the compulsory insurance threshold of 84,150 EUR?
  • Which additional contribution rate does each employee's health insurer charge for 2027, compared with the 2.9 percent average?
  • How are bonuses and one-off payments treated against the annual ceilings in the month they are paid?
  • Which levies and company pension contributions apply on top of the four main branches in your specific setup?

The answers turn the illustrative figures above into a number your CFO can sign off.

A note on the comparison

Rising Lohnnebenkosten are sometimes used as a blanket argument for external capacity. That shortcut is unreliable. An external engagement that is in substance an employment relationship can trigger the very contributions it was meant to avoid, plus arrears, under §7 SGB IV and §266a StGB. The economic benefit of non-payroll capacity only exists when the contract is a real Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag with the provider directing its own people. This is general information, not legal or tax advice, and the figures should be checked against the final published values and your payroll provider.
