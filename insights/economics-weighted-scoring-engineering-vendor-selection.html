Weighted Scoring for Engineering Vendor Selection: Price vs Quality

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When an engineering vendor is selected by procurement, the decision often comes down to the lowest daily rate, because price is the only criterion that is easy to compare. For engineering services that is a poor proxy for value: the cheapest offer can be the most expensive once rework, coordination effort and delays are counted. The answer is a weighted scoring model, known in German procurement as Nutzwertanalyse, agreed between the CTO and Einkauf before offers are opened, with knock-out criteria, explicit weights and a price formula that does not drown out quality.

Why a structured method is worth the effort

The market for software and engineering services is large and diverse. Bitkom forecast in July 2026 that the German software market will grow 9.9 percent to EUR 58.1 billion in 2026, while IT services grow 3.1 percent. A buyer faces many offers with very different delivery models, and a comparison by rate alone hides those differences.

Procurement teams also see the gap. In the BME benchmark "Top-Kennzahlen im Einkauf 2026", 93 percent of companies saw high or very high potential for AI in the analysis and evaluation of offers. That points to the same problem: evaluating offers well is hard, and most companies feel they are not yet doing it systematically. A clear scoring model is the foundation any tool would need anyway.

For some buyers the method is mandatory. Public-sector and sector contracting entities, such as municipal utilities, must follow procurement law. From 1 January 2026 the EU thresholds for supplies and services are EUR 216,000 for most contracting authorities, EUR 140,000 for federal authorities and EUR 432,000 for sector contracting entities, as summarised by CMS. Above them, transparent award criteria and weightings are required in any case.

Step 1: define knock-out criteria

Knock-out criteria are minimum conditions. An offer that fails one is excluded, regardless of price. Typical examples for an engineering vendor:

  • Willingness to sign an Auftragsverarbeitungsvertrag under Art. 28 DSGVO with Standardvertragsklauseln.
  • Acceptance of a contract structure without Arbeitnehmerüberlassung, with a Leistungsbeschreibung and Abnahme.
  • Source code and credentials held in the buyer's own accounts.
  • A named contact person who can communicate with the buyer's team in the required language.
  • No unresolved export control issue for the intended work.

Step 2: set weights before opening offers

Weights express what matters. They must be fixed before anyone sees prices, otherwise they drift towards justifying a preferred result. A reasonable starting point for an engineering services contract:

CriterionWeightWhat is assessed
Price30 %Total cost for the defined scope over the contract term
Delivery approach25 %Sprint structure, acceptance criteria, quality gates, risk handling
Team and communication20 %Seniority, continuity, language, single point of contact
Security and compliance15 %Information security, data protection, contract model
Exit and transition10 %Documentation, handover obligations, notice periods

Each non-price criterion is scored on a defined scale, for example 0 to 10, with written anchor descriptions for what a 2, a 5 and an 8 look like. Anchors make the scores of different reviewers comparable.

Step 3: choose a price formula carefully

The price formula has a large and often overlooked effect. The common ratio formula (lowest price divided by offered price, times 10) gives a reasonable spread. Formulas that award zero points at a fixed distance from the lowest price can make price dominate even at a 30 percent weight. Whatever formula you use, test it with plausible numbers before publishing it.

Illustrative example

This is an illustrative calculation with invented offers. Three vendors quote a monthly fee for the same scope: A at EUR 30,000, B at EUR 26,000, C at EUR 36,000. With the ratio formula, price scores are A 8.7, B 10.0 and C 7.2.

Criterion (weight)Vendor AVendor BVendor C
Price (30 %)8.710.07.2
Delivery approach (25 %)869
Team and communication (20 %)859
Security and compliance (15 %)769
Exit and transition (10 %)848
Weighted score8.056.808.37

The cheapest offer ranks last. The most expensive offer ranks first, narrowly ahead of A. The result is not that price does not matter, but that it matters in proportion to the weight the company agreed it should have.

Common mistakes

  • Scoring the sales presentation instead of the team. Ask who will actually work on the engagement and score them.
  • Comparing day rates for different scopes. Price must refer to the same defined scope and term, otherwise the comparison is meaningless.
  • Too many criteria. More than six or seven main criteria dilute the weights until no single one decides anything.

Step 4: document and sense-check

  1. Record each reviewer's scores and a one-line rationale per criterion.
  2. Run a sensitivity check: would the ranking change if the price weight moved by ten points? If yes, discuss that explicitly before deciding.
  3. Verify the top-ranked offer in a structured conversation with the proposed team, not only the sales contact.
  4. Keep the scoring file. It is the documentation Einkauf, auditors and, for public entities, review bodies will ask for.

For public and sector contracting entities, procurement law sets additional requirements for award criteria and their publication. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice.
