Germany Loses 4.3 Million Workers by 2036: An Engineering Capacity Plan

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

The 2026 IT hiring market feels easier than it did three years ago, and many CTOs are adjusting their plans accordingly. That reading is correct for the next twelve months and misleading for the next ten. The structural driver of engineering scarcity in Germany is not the business cycle but demographics, and the demographic curve is getting steeper, not flatter. A capacity plan that runs beyond 2027 should treat the current softness as a window to build durable access to engineers, not as evidence that the shortage is over.

What the latest demographic data says

In June 2026 the Institut der deutschen Wirtschaft (IW Köln) published an updated projection: by 2036 Germany will have about 4.3 million fewer people of working age than today. Two years earlier the same institute had estimated a gap of 3 million. The revision is driven by two factors. Roughly 1.3 million people reach retirement age every year, while only about 800.000 enter working age, which leaves a net loss of around 500.000 potential workers annually. At the same time net migration has fallen sharply, so the offset that kept the labour force stable in recent years is weakening.

IW Köln expects total labour potential to fall by about 7 percent to roughly 51 million people by 2036. The population itself declined by about 100.000 in 2025, and the institute now expects it to fall below 82 million by 2040. These are not IT figures, but they set the ceiling for every occupation, including software and systems engineering.

Why the cyclical picture points the other way

The short-term labour market data looks almost relaxed. The Bundesagentur für Arbeit reports about 1,61 million people employed in IT and ICT occupations in 2025, with around 54.000 unemployed people seeking IT work, an increase of 27 percent on the previous year. Reported vacancies averaged roughly 13.000 and declined. Bitkom's September 2026 labour market study counts 79.000 missing IT specialists, down from 149.000 in 2023.

Yet the same Bitkom survey shows that 76 percent of companies expect the shortage to worsen again, and 97 percent of companies with open IT positions report difficulty filling them. The contradiction resolves once you separate cycle from structure. Weak demand in 2025 and 2026 has temporarily released capacity. The retirement wave keeps removing experienced engineers regardless of demand, and the people who leave are disproportionately the ones who know the legacy systems, the plant interfaces and the customer-specific code.

A four-step method for a ten-year engineering capacity plan

Most engineering headcount plans are built bottom-up from next year's roadmap. That is necessary, but it hides the demographic exposure. The following method adds a structural layer on top of the roadmap plan.

  1. Map the age profile of critical knowledge. List the systems and domains your product depends on and name the people who can change them safely. For each person, note the expected exit window. In Mittelstand companies this list often shows that two or three people over 58 hold the only working knowledge of a core system.
  2. Estimate replacement lead time per role. Separate roles you can fill from the open market within one quarter from roles that take two to four quarters, such as embedded, safety-relevant, SAP or security engineering. The second group is where demographic loss turns into delivery risk.
  3. Model three sourcing channels. For each critical role, decide which share you will cover by internal hiring, by international recruitment and relocation, and by external capacity delivered remotely. Put numbers on each channel, including time to productivity.
  4. Fix the review cadence. Review the plan twice a year against two indicators: your own exit calendar and an external market signal such as the Bitkom shortage figure or the Bundesagentur vacancy data.

No single channel closes a structural gap of this size. The point of the table is to make the mix explicit, so that the board sees a deliberate portfolio rather than an open requisition list.

Use the cooling market to lock in access

The paradox of 2026 is that the best moment to prepare for structural scarcity is a period of cyclical slack. Salaries are rising more slowly, candidates are more available, and external partners have capacity to onboard carefully. Three actions are worth taking now.

  • Hire for succession, not only for roadmap. Where a critical system depends on one person close to retirement, budget a successor with a twelve-month overlap.
  • Document before people leave. Architecture decision records, runbooks and recorded walkthroughs cost little compared with reverse-engineering a system after its owner has retired.
  • Test an external channel at small scale. A bounded scope with defined acceptance criteria tells you within two or three months whether remote capacity can carry part of your load when the market tightens again.

What to put in front of the board

A demographic capacity plan fits on one page: the list of critical knowledge holders with exit windows, the replacement lead time per role family, the intended sourcing mix for 2027 to 2030, and the two indicators you will track. That page converts an abstract national statistic into a specific risk register for your company, and it moves the discussion from whether the shortage is back to how much exposure the company is willing to carry.
