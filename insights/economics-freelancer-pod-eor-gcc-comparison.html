Freelancer vs Managed Pod vs EOR vs Own GCC in India: Cost Comparison

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When a DACH scaleup or Mittelstand IT department needs engineering capacity it cannot hire locally, four models usually reach the shortlist: German freelancers, a managed delivery pod, an Employer of Record (EOR) in India, or an own Global Capability Centre (GCC). They are often compared on headline rate alone. That is a mistake, because each model shifts a different set of costs and risks to the client. This guide compares them on five dimensions: direct cost, hidden cost, legal exposure in Germany, control, and time to productive output.

The four models in one sentence each

  • Freelancer: an independent contractor, usually German-based, billed per hour or day.
  • Managed pod: a small team supplied by a service provider who owns the delivery result or service, under a Werkvertrag (BGB §631) or Dienstvertrag (BGB §611).
  • EOR in India: a platform legally employs engineers in India on your behalf; you direct their daily work and pay salary plus a platform fee.
  • Own GCC: you incorporate an Indian subsidiary and build a team as a direct employer.

Direct cost: what the published figures show

For freelancers, the freelancermap Freelancer-Kompass 2026 reports a median hourly rate of EUR 90 for software and web development, and EUR 120 for SAP and ERP specialists. At 200 billable days of eight hours, a software freelancer at the median costs about EUR 144,000 per year.

For EOR, published pricing comparisons for India list platform fees of roughly USD 199 to 699 per employee per month, on top of the Indian salary, statutory contributions and equipment. The fee looks small. The salary component depends heavily on seniority and city, and self-reported compensation data for senior engineers in Bengaluru spans a wide range, with multinational employers at the top end.

For a managed pod, the price is a contracted fee per month or per sprint, covering the team, delivery management and the provider's overhead. For a GCC, there is no external price at all: you carry entity setup, local directors, accounting, transfer pricing documentation, office, HR and recruiting as your own costs.

The supply picture on both sides

Why four models instead of simply hiring? In Germany, Bitkom counted about 109,000 unfilled IT positions in 2025, with an average of 7.7 months to fill one. Switzerland faces a similar gap: ICT-Berufsbildung Schweiz projected in 2025 that the country will lack about 54,400 ICT specialists by 2033. India sits at the other end of the equation. NASSCOM estimates the Indian technology workforce at about 5.95 million in fiscal year 2026, after 135,000 net additions, and a 2024 NASSCOM and Zinnov study counted more than 1,700 Global Capability Centres employing about 1.9 million people. That last figure matters for the GCC option: a new captive centre competes for engineers with a large, established GCC sector, which can push up both salaries and attrition risk in the main hubs.

Hidden cost by model

Freelancers

The main hidden cost is legal. If a freelancer works integrated into your team, takes daily instructions and has no entrepreneurial risk, the Deutsche Rentenversicherung may classify the relationship as dependent employment under SGB IV §7. Consequences can include retroactive social security contributions and, in serious cases, criminal exposure under §266a StGB. A Statusfeststellungsverfahren under SGB IV §7a gives certainty but takes time. Availability is the second hidden cost: senior freelancers in niche stacks are often booked months ahead.

Managed pods

The hidden cost here is specification. A provider can only take delivery responsibility if the scope is written down: a Leistungsbeschreibung with acceptance criteria and formal Abnahme under BGB §640. If the client instead directs individual engineers day to day, the arrangement can drift toward Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 AÜG, with the consequences set out in §§9 and 10 AÜG. A well-run pod requires a client-side product owner who writes clear sprint goals.

EOR in India

An EOR removes the need for an Indian entity, but not the need for management. You recruit, onboard, direct and retain the engineers yourself, across time zones, without a local delivery lead. Turnover in competitive Indian tech markets falls on you. Depending on how the engineers work and whether they conclude contracts for you, tax advisers may also need to assess permanent establishment risk.

Own GCC

A captive centre gives maximum control and long-term cost advantages at scale, but the fixed overhead is substantial and the first productive output can take many months. It generally makes sense only when a company is confident it will sustain a large team in India for years.

A decision framework

  1. Is the need under six months or highly specialised? A freelancer may fit, with a clean project scope and a status check.
  2. Can you describe outcomes per sprint but lack local delivery management? A managed pod fits.
  3. Do you have strong engineering management with time to lead a remote team directly? EOR can work.
  4. Do you plan a large, permanent Indian presence? Evaluate a GCC, ideally after proving the delivery model with a smaller setup.

Combinations are the norm

The models rarely exclude each other. A common pattern: the core team in Germany owns architecture and product decisions, a managed pod delivers clearly bounded work packages, and individual freelancers cover short specialist needs. Each model must still run by its own rules. Freelancers need an independent scope, the pod needs a Leistungsbeschreibung with Abnahme, and employees get normal line management. Problems start when all three groups receive individual instructions from the same person in one daily stand-up, because that blurs the boundaries each contract model depends on.

How to compare on equal terms

Convert every option into cost per productive engineering day, including the client-side management time each model requires. A freelancer at EUR 720 per day who needs four hours a week of senior guidance, and an EOR engineer who needs a full-time remote lead, are not cheaper than they look on paper until that time is priced in. A managed pod that bundles delivery management into the fee makes the comparison simpler, but only if the scope is specified properly.
