DRV Betriebsprüfung: How Auditors Review Your IT Contractor Spend

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Every employer in Germany is audited by the Deutsche Rentenversicherung (DRV) at least once every four years under §28p SGB IV. For a technology company, the most consequential part of that routine Betriebsprüfung is rarely payroll itself. It is the ledger of payments to freelancers and external vendors, where auditors look for people who should have been on payroll. The answer is not to avoid external capacity but to make the contractor side of your books readable: clear vendor categories, deliverable-based invoices and a short written rationale for each long-running engagement.

How the routine audit works

According to the DRV's own description of its KIRA project, the pension insurance carries out about 400.000 Betriebsprüfungen per year with roughly 1.700 auditors, which leaves on average less than one working day per audit. Annual back-payments are described by the DRV as being in the high hundreds of millions of euros. The regional carrier DRV Westfalen reported for 2025 that contributions of about 31,2 million EUR were reclaimed in its area alone, out of a cycle covering more than 134.000 businesses.

The time constraint explains the method. Auditors cannot reconstruct every working relationship. They work from data: payroll records transmitted electronically, financial accounting extracts and, increasingly, automated anomaly detection. KIRA (Künstliche Intelligenz für risikoorientierte Arbeitgeberprüfungen) is being developed by DRV Bund with funding from the Federal Ministry of Labour to flag unusual patterns in employer data and show auditors where to spend their limited time. Noerr has commented that this AI support is likely to uncover more cases of Scheinselbständigkeit.

What auditors look for in contractor spend

The legal test is §7 Abs. 1 SGB IV: employment is indicated by work under instructions and integration into the organisation of the instructing party. In an audit, this test is applied to the accounts. The following patterns tend to draw attention because they are visible in bookkeeping data without any interview:

  • Individuals on fee accounts. Payments to natural persons booked as "Fremdleistungen" or "Beratung", recurring monthly.
  • Constant amounts. Invoices that vary only with the number of working days, like a salary.
  • Duration. Engagements that run for years without a change in scope.
  • Former employees. People who left payroll and reappear as contractors doing the same job.
  • Missing vendor substance. One-person "companies" without their own staff, website or other clients.

Once a case is selected, the auditor requests contracts, invoices and sometimes questionnaires to the contractor. If the facts point to employment, the DRV assesses contributions for up to four years, and under §25 SGB IV up to thirty years where contributions were withheld intentionally, plus late-payment surcharges under §24 SGB IV.

The Statusfeststellung route and its statistics

Companies that are unsure about a specific engagement can request a binding decision through the Statusfeststellungsverfahren under §7a SGB IV. According to a Bundestag reply reported by the parliamentary press service in August 2025, the Clearingstelle handled 23.052 such procedures in 2024. In the first half of 2025, of the decisions that classified the relationship either way, 5.146 found dependent employment and 7.706 found self-employment, which means roughly 40 percent ended in an employment finding.

That figure is a useful reality check. Even among cases that parties choose to submit, presumably because they believe in self-employment, a large share is classified as employment. For engagements structured as individual freelancers embedded in a product team, the odds in a routine audit are unlikely to be better.

Preparing the contractor side of the ledger

A practical preparation takes a finance lead and an engineering lead about one working day per quarter. The goal is that each external cost line can be explained in one sentence and backed by a document.

1. Separate the accountsBook companies delivering defined services separately from payments to individualsTwo clean ledgers instead of one mixed account
2. List individualsFor every natural person paid more than three months in a row, record role, start date and contract typeAn individual contractor list

Why vendor structure matters more than contract wording

Auditors and courts look at how the work is actually done. A contract titled "Werkvertrag" does not protect an arrangement in which your engineering manager distributes tickets to an individual every morning. Conversely, a company that delivers through its own team, under its own lead, against a written Leistungsbeschreibung and with formal acceptance under §640 BGB creates a different data trail: invoices from a legal entity with staff, linked to sprints, and no individual on your fee accounts at all.

This is also the structural difference between staffing individual offshore developers and contracting a managed team. With individual developers, the status question has to be answered person by person. With a vendor-led team, the main question becomes whether the vendor really steers its people, which is a question of process and can be documented. A short checklist for the next audit notice

  • Is there a current list of every natural person paid as a contractor in the last four years?
  • Can each long-running engagement be justified with a scope document and acceptance records?
  • Have former employees been rehired as contractors, and if so, has their role changed?
  • Are vendor invoices tied to deliverables rather than hours alone?
  • Is there a named person who can answer auditor questions within days, not weeks?
