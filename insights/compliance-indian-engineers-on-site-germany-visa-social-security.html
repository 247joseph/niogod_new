Indian Engineers On Site in Germany: Visa, Social Security and AÜG

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Even fully remote engineering engagements need occasional presence: a kickoff workshop in Munich, a commissioning week at a plant near Stuttgart, an architecture review with the client's team. When engineers employed by an Indian vendor travel to Germany for this, three regimes meet at once: immigration law, social security coordination and the AÜG. Getting one of them wrong can turn a two-week visit into a compliance problem. The answer is to plan on-site visits as defined, short and vendor-led activities, with the right visa category, a certificate of coverage and a clear line between meeting the client and working inside the client's organisation.

The context: more Indian professionals, more mobility

Indian professionals are now a significant part of Germany's technical workforce. According to a 2025 research paper by the Stiftung Wissenschaft und Politik (SWP), the number of Indian nationals in Germany roughly tripled from about 86.000 in 2015 to about 280.000 in 2025. Labour migration routes are shifting: EU Blue Cards issued to Indians fell from 8.405 in 2022 to 3.276 in 2024, while the new Opportunity Card proved popular. Demand remains: Bitkom's 2026 study estimates about 79.000 missing IT specialists, and 47 percent of companies name insufficient German skills as a hurdle when hiring. Remote delivery from India with targeted on-site visits is one way companies combine both realities.

Immigration: business visit or work

For short stays, Indian nationals need a Schengen visa, which allows stays of up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Frequent business travellers can receive multiple-entry visas valid for several years, as the Berlin immigration authority notes, provided each stay respects the 90/180-day rule.

The key question is the purpose of the visit. The Beschäftigungsverordnung treats certain short business activities as not constituting employment, for example attending meetings, negotiating or preparing contracts, and consultations on behalf of a foreign employer. Productive work in Germany, such as writing code inside the client's team for weeks or operating client systems on site, generally requires a residence title that permits employment. Where the line runs in a specific case should be checked with an immigration lawyer before the visa application, and the invitation letter should describe the actual purpose truthfully.

Social security: the Indo-German agreement

Germany and India signed a social security agreement on 8 October 2008, which took effect on 1 October 2009, according to the German missions in India. Under the agreement, employees sent temporarily by their employer to the other country remain subject to their home country's rules for up to 48 months. The Deutsche Rentenversicherung notes that the agreement covers pension insurance only and that the posting is documented with a certificate of coverage. For assignments that exceed 48 months, an exception agreement must be requested before work begins.

For short visits, this means the Indian engineer stays in the Indian system, provided the vendor obtains the certificate from the Indian authority. The German client should ask for a copy before travel. Whether German rules for other branches of social security can apply depends on the facts of the posting, which is another reason to keep visits short and clearly tied to the Indian employment relationship.

The AÜG trap on site

The most underestimated risk is labour law. If an engineer employed by an Indian vendor works in a German client's office, follows the client's instructions and is integrated into its team, this can be Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 AÜG. A foreign vendor without a German AÜG licence then triggers the consequences of §9 and §10 AÜG: the contract with the vendor is void, and an employment relationship with the German client may be deemed to exist. On-site presence makes integration visible and easy to prove.

The safeguards are straightforward:

  • The on-site purpose is defined in writing in advance (workshop, review, acceptance), with dates and participants.
  • The vendor's own lead or Projektleiter is present or remains the instruction channel during the visit.
  • The visiting engineer does not receive client email accounts, badges as staff or a place in internal team structures.
  • Results are handed over and accepted through the normal Abnahme process under §640 BGB.

Tax: keep track of days

Under the Germany-India double taxation agreement, employment income of a short-term visitor generally remains taxable in India if the stay does not exceed 183 days in the relevant period and the remuneration is not borne by a German employer or permanent establishment. Repeated or long visits by the same engineers can also raise the question of whether the Indian vendor has a permanent establishment in Germany. Both points are rarely an issue for occasional workshops but should be monitored with a simple travel log.

A pre-travel checklist

  1. Purpose, dates and agenda documented and agreed between client and vendor.
  2. Visa category checked against the planned activities; invitation letter reflects the actual purpose.
  3. Certificate of coverage under the social security agreement obtained by the vendor.
  4. Instruction channel through the vendor's lead confirmed; no integration into client staff structures.
  5. Travel days logged per engineer for tax monitoring.
