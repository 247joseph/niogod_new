Nearshore (Poland, Romania, Portugal) vs Offshore India: A DACH Comparison

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Most comparisons of nearshore Europe and offshore India are written by vendors who sell one of the two, and they read accordingly. For a DACH engineering leader the question is not which region is better in general. It is which constraint binds hardest on the work in front of you: real-time collaboration, regulatory simplicity, depth of a specific skill, or cost per delivered outcome. Poland, Romania and Portugal win clearly on some of these. India wins clearly on others. The honest answer is a decision by workload, not by geography.

The numbers behind the comparison

Cost and talent supply are the two variables that move fastest, and both can be measured.

  • Labour cost. According to Eurostat data reported by Destatis, an hour of work cost an average of EUR 45 in Germany and EUR 46.30 in Austria in 2025, against an EU average of EUR 34.90. Romania stood at EUR 13.60. The nearshore cost advantage is real but narrowing: labour costs in Poland rose 10.5 percent in 2025, compared with 3.6 percent in Germany.
  • European talent pool. Eurostat counts 10.45 million ICT specialists in the EU in 2025, 5.0 percent of total employment. Growth slowed to 2.6 percent, from 4.5 percent a year earlier. Romania has one of the lowest ICT shares of employment in the EU, at 2.7 percent.
  • India. Nasscom's February 2026 strategic review puts the direct workforce of India's technology industry at 5.95 million in FY26, with 135,000 net additions during the year.

These are economy-wide averages, not engineering rates, and they should not be read as a price list. They show the direction of travel: nearshore labour costs are converging with Western Europe, while the Indian pool keeps growing in absolute terms.

The structural differences that do not change

Some differences are facts of geography and law, and no vendor promise removes them.

The time zone point deserves precision. A 3.5 to 4.5 hour offset is not a night shift problem. It leaves a working overlap of roughly four to five hours with a standard German day, and it gives a delivery team a morning of focused work before the German day starts. Whether that is an advantage or a handicap depends on how much of your process requires synchronous conversation.

Where nearshore is the stronger choice

  • Synchronous, high-churn work. If requirements change daily, your product owner works through live pairing, and decisions happen in hallway-style calls, a zero to one hour difference matters.
  • On-site presence. Factory acceptance tests, customer workshops in Stuttgart or Linz, hardware-in-the-loop labs that cannot be replicated remotely. Short travel lowers the friction.
  • Data that must stay in the EU. Some customers, especially in the public sector and parts of healthcare, contractually exclude any third-country access. Nearshore avoids the question entirely.
  • Small, short engagements. For a three-month project the setup cost of a transfer impact assessment and a new contractual framework may not be worth it.

Where India is the stronger choice

  • Depth of the talent pool. India hosts roughly 1,700 global capability centres, about 53 percent of the global total according to Vestian's 2025 estimate. That density means experienced engineers in AI/ML, data engineering, embedded and enterprise platforms in numbers no single nearshore country matches.
  • Sustained capacity over years. For multi-year platform work, the size of the market makes it easier to scale a team or replace a member without restarting a search.
  • Specified, asynchronous delivery. When work is described as deliverables with acceptance criteria, the time offset becomes a handover window rather than a barrier.
  • Cost per outcome. Nearshore rates in the major Central European markets have risen substantially over the past decade as those markets matured. India still generally offers more senior capacity per euro, though the gap at senior level is smaller than many buyers assume.

The cost comparison most buyers get wrong

Comparing day rates across regions is misleading because it ignores management overhead on your side. An inexpensive team that needs a German engineering manager to translate requirements, review every pull request and chase status is not inexpensive. A useful comparison uses fully loaded cost per accepted deliverable.

Illustrative calculation: suppose Option A costs EUR 30,000 per month and requires 40 percent of an internal lead's time. Option B costs EUR 36,000 per month and requires 10 percent, because the vendor provides its own project lead and quality review. If that internal lead costs EUR 12,000 per month fully loaded, Option A is EUR 34,800 and Option B is EUR 37,200. The gap narrows from EUR 6,000 to EUR 2,400, and that is before counting the roadmap work the lead could do with the freed time. These are hypothetical figures; use your own.

A decision framework by workload

  1. Classify the work. Exploratory and synchronous, or specifiable and outcome-based?
  2. Classify the data. Does it include personal data or customer data with contractual location restrictions? If yes, check whether EU-only processing is required or whether SCCs plus technical measures suffice.
  3. Classify the duration. Under six months, or a multi-year capability?
  4. Check the skill. Is the required stack common, or rare enough that pool depth dominates?
  5. Check your interface capacity. Who on your side owns the relationship, and how many hours per week can they give?

Exploratory, short, EU-restricted work points to nearshore. Specified, long-running, skill-deep work points to India. Many DACH companies end up with both, which is a reasonable result if each is used for what it does well.

Contract structure matters in both regions

Geography does not decide whether an engagement is a Werkvertrag under BGB §631 or de facto Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under AÜG §1. Integration into your organisation and day-to-day instruction by your managers do. A nearshore team in Kraków managed hour by hour by your staff carries the same structural risk as an offshore team in Bangalore managed the same way. A vendor-side lead between your team and the engineers addresses this regardless of region. This article is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Whichever region fits the workload, the result depends on who steers the engineers and how their output is accepted. niogod™ delivers from India with a German-speaking Projektleiter on the vendor side, under a Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag with Abnahme per sprint (see the pod structure). If you are comparing regions for next year's roadmap, it is worth comparing structures at the same time.
