EU AI Act After the Digital Omnibus: Revised Deadlines for Engineering

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

The EU AI Act timetable that most engineering roadmaps were built on no longer applies. With the Digital Omnibus on AI, the EU moved the high-risk obligations for stand-alone systems from August 2026 to December 2027, and for AI embedded in regulated products from August 2027 to August 2028. For CTOs, the temptation is to treat this as a free year. The better reading is that the deadline moved but the engineering work did not get smaller: risk management, data governance, logging and technical documentation still have to be built into the product, and the extra time is only useful if it is planned now.

What the Omnibus changed, and what it did not

According to Gibson Dunn's analysis of the provisional agreement of 6 May 2026, the main changes are:

  • Annex III high-risk systems (stand-alone use cases such as employment, credit scoring or critical infrastructure): obligations apply from 2 December 2027 instead of 2 August 2026.
  • Annex I high-risk systems (AI as a safety component of products under EU harmonisation law, such as machinery or medical devices): obligations apply from 2 August 2028 instead of 2 August 2027.
  • Transparency under Article 50 remains on schedule from 2 August 2026, with a grace period until 2 December 2026 for the watermarking duty in Article 50(2) for systems placed on the market before August 2026.
  • AI literacy under Article 4 is softened: providers and deployers must support the development of AI literacy among their staff rather than ensure a specific level.

Cuatrecasas reports that the Council gave its final approval on 29 June 2026, with entry into force three days after publication in the Official Journal. The prohibitions in force since February 2025 and the obligations for general-purpose AI models since August 2025 were not postponed. A new prohibition on systems that generate non-consensual intimate images of real people was added, applying from December 2026.

Why the extension is narrower than it looks

The postponement was justified by missing harmonised standards and guidance, not by a change in substance. The requirements of Articles 9 to 15 (risk management, data and data governance, technical documentation, record keeping, transparency to deployers, human oversight, accuracy and robustness) remain as written. They are design properties. Logging that was not designed into a system cannot be documented into it afterwards, and training data provenance that was never recorded cannot be reconstructed for a conformity assessment.

The penalty framework of Article 99 also remains: up to 35 million EUR or 7 percent of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices, and up to 15 million EUR or 3 percent for most other infringements, including high-risk obligations.

How German companies see it

Bitkom's 2026 survey of 603 companies, published in September 2026, found that 57 percent of German companies now use AI, up from 36 percent a year earlier and 20 percent two years earlier. The share that considers itself affected by the AI Act rose from 23 percent in 2025 to 37 percent in 2026. At the same time, 70 percent offer AI training to their staff. The picture is of rapid adoption with growing regulatory awareness, which makes a structured plan for the next 24 months more valuable than a wait-and-see stance.

A revised timeline for engineering teams

Since 2 August 2025General-purpose AI model obligationsRelevant if you train or substantially modify foundation models
2 August 2026Article 50 transparencyDisclosure of AI interaction, labelling of synthetic content
2 December 2026Watermarking for legacy systems, new prohibitionMarking of generated output for systems already on the market
2 December 2027Annex III high-riskRisk management, data governance, logging, documentation, oversight
2 August 2028Annex I high-risk (product-embedded)AI Act requirements integrated into product conformity, for example under MDR or the Machinery Regulation

Using the extra time: a four-phase plan

  1. Classify (now to Q1 2027). List every AI component in your products and internal tools, assign it to prohibited, high-risk (Annex I or III), limited-risk or minimal-risk, and record the reasoning. Decide where you act as provider and where as deployer.
  2. Retrofit the foundations (Q1 to Q3 2027). Introduce data lineage for training and evaluation sets, structured event logging that satisfies Article 12, model versioning and reproducible training pipelines.
  3. Document (Q3 2027 onwards). Build the technical documentation in Annex IV as a living artefact in the repository, updated with every release, rather than as a one-off document.
  4. Assess and monitor. Prepare the conformity assessment route, post-market monitoring and incident reporting. For Annex I products, align with the existing notified body process.

What this means when external teams build the system

When an external engineering team writes or trains parts of a high-risk system, the obligations stay with the provider who places the system on the market under its own name. The contract with the external team should therefore specify which artefacts it produces: data lineage records, logging implementation, test reports, model cards and the relevant parts of the Annex IV documentation. In a Werkvertrag with acceptance under §640 BGB, these artefacts can be made part of the acceptance criteria for each sprint, so that compliance evidence is produced continuously rather than assembled under pressure in late 2027. Data handling for training sets also has to respect the GDPR, including Article 28 and the transfer rules in Articles 44 and following when data is processed outside the EU.
