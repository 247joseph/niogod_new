The Cost of Reversing an Engineering Hire in Germany

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Every permanent engineering hire in Germany carries an option that most budgets never price: the cost of reversing it. When a roadmap changes, a funding round slips or a role turns out to be the wrong one, ending the employment relationship involves notice periods, often a severance payment, legal costs and a gap in capacity. None of this argues against hiring. It argues for knowing the exit cost before the decision, especially for roles tied to a single project or to a funding milestone. This analysis sets out the legal framework in general terms, the current litigation trend, and a calculation method for the exit cost of an engineering role.

The legal framework in brief

During an agreed probation period of up to six months, either side can terminate with two weeks' notice under §622 Abs. 3 BGB. After that, two thresholds change the picture. Under §1 Abs. 1 KSchG, the Kündigungsschutzgesetz applies once the employment has lasted more than six months, and under §23 Abs. 1 KSchG it applies in establishments with more than ten employees, counted on a full-time equivalent basis. Most Series A and B scaleups pass that threshold early. From then on, a dismissal requires a social justification, typically operational, conduct-related or person-related reasons.

Statutory notice periods for the employer increase with tenure under §622 Abs. 2 BGB, from one month to the end of a calendar month after two years to seven months after twenty years. Employment contracts often provide longer periods. Where a works council exists, it must be heard before any dismissal under §102 BetrVG.

German law does not grant a general right to severance. In practice, however, most contested dismissals end in a settlement with a payment. §1a KSchG provides a reference point: for an operational dismissal, the employee can claim half a monthly salary per year of employment if the employer offers it in the dismissal letter and the employee lets the three-week deadline for filing a claim pass. Negotiated settlements frequently use a factor between 0.5 and 1.0 monthly salaries per year, with higher factors where the dismissal is legally weak. In first-instance labour court proceedings, each party bears its own lawyer's fees under §12a ArbGG, regardless of the outcome.

The current trend

Dismissal disputes have become more frequent. The legal expenses insurer ARAG reported in July 2026 that dismissal protection claims in its portfolio rose by 33 percent in 2025 compared with the previous year, and by a further 9.8 percent in the first half of 2026. Labour law cases overall have risen by 63 percent since 2021, according to the same source.

On the employee side, behaviour is less confrontational than often assumed. The HR Works Kündigungsreport 2026, based on a survey of about 6,000 employees in April 2026, found that 62 percent of employees who received a severance offer accepted the first offer without negotiation. At the same time, 47 percent of dismissed employees received no financial compensation at all. Of those who negotiated, 54 percent hired a lawyer and 32 percent of those cases ended in court. Technical specialists with good market prospects tend to negotiate more, not less.

A worked example

The following is an illustrative calculation for a senior engineer with a gross salary of 96,000 EUR (8,000 EUR per month), three years of tenure and a contract that follows the statutory notice period. It assumes an operational dismissal resolved by settlement.

ComponentAssumptionIllustrative cost
Notice periodAbout 1.5 months including time to the end of the month, with garden leave12,000 EUR
Employer on-costs during noticeAbout 19 percent2,280 EUR
Severance0.75 monthly salaries per year, 3 years18,000 EUR
Own legal feesAdvice and settlement negotiation5,000 EUR
Internal timeHR, management, handover, 40 hours at 90 EUR3,600 EUR
Total direct cost40,880 EUR

In this example, reversing the hire costs about 43 percent of an annual salary in direct cost alone. It excludes the acquisition cost already sunk when the person was hired, the knowledge that leaves with them, and the effect on the remaining team. If the dismissal is contested and the court finds it unjustified, back pay for the period of the proceedings can be added, which is why settlements are the norm.

What drives the number up

  • Tenure. Both notice and severance grow with years of employment.
  • Weak justification. An operational dismissal requires that the role genuinely ceases and that a social selection among comparable employees is carried out. Small teams with overlapping roles make this harder, and settlement factors rise accordingly.
  • Special protection. Employees on parental leave, severely disabled employees and works council members have additional protection that can make a dismissal impractical.
  • Contractual extras. Longer contractual notice periods, unvested equity that accelerates, and non-compete clauses with compensation under §74 HGB all add cost.

How to use the exit cost in planning

  1. Estimate it before the hire. For roles tied to one project or one customer, calculate the exit cost at year two and year three and add it to the business case.
  2. Match the instrument to the horizon. Fixed-term contracts without objective reason are limited under the TzBfG to two years. Work with a defined end is often better served by a service contract than by employment.
  3. Plan restructuring with advice. Social selection, works council hearing and mass dismissal notification thresholds under §17 KSchG require legal preparation, not a template.
  4. Hold the exit cost in reserve when runway is short. A scaleup with twelve months of runway should know what it would cost to adjust the engineering team if the next round is delayed.

This article is general information, not legal advice. Specific dismissals should always be reviewed by a specialist labour lawyer before any step is taken.
