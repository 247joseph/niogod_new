Rahmenvertrag and Call-Off Orders: Structuring Engineering Capacity

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Companies that buy engineering capacity more than once tend to run into the same problem: every new need triggers a full procurement cycle, with a new contract, a new legal review and a new approval. The standard answer in German procurement practice is a two-level structure: a Rahmenvertrag that fixes the legal and commercial terms once, and individual call-off orders, often called Einzelabrufe or Leistungsscheine, that define each concrete scope. Done well, this lets engineering add or change work packages in days rather than months. Done badly, it turns into a staffing arrangement that creates legal risk. This guide explains how to split content between the two levels, which commercial levers to use, and where the guardrails are.

Why the structure matters now

The market for external engineering is in a phase where buyers have more leverage than in recent years. Lünendonk reported in June 2026 that the German IT services market grew organically by only 2.9 percent in 2025, and that employment at IT service providers fell by 0.6 percent, the first decline in more than twenty years. Benchmark data from Metrics, published by CIO magazine in March 2026, showed top-tier consultant day rates falling by 4.6 percent. At the same time, 37 percent of CIOs told Lünendonk they plan to increase spending on external providers, and providers expect their data and AI business to grow by 24.2 percent in 2026. Buyers who want to lock in current terms while keeping flexibility for shifting priorities are well served by a framework.

What belongs in the Rahmenvertrag

The framework agreement holds everything that does not change between work packages.

  • Contract type and governance. Whether call-offs are Werkvertrag deliverables under §631 BGB, Dienstvertrag services under §611 BGB, or either, and how each type is accepted or confirmed.
  • Pricing logic. Rate card per role, or a fixed monthly fee per team unit, plus the mechanism for adjusting prices during the term.
  • Volume terms. Any minimum commitment, volume tiers and the consequences of falling below or exceeding them.
  • Ramp rules. Notice periods for scaling capacity up and down, separate from termination of the framework itself.
  • Rights and obligations. Usage rights and IP transfer, confidentiality, liability caps, insurance.
  • Data protection and security. The data processing agreement under DSGVO Art. 28, transfer mechanisms under Art. 44 ff. for providers outside the EU, and security requirements.
  • Invoicing and payment. Currency, invoice content, PO references, payment terms and tax treatment.
  • Exit. Handover obligations, documentation standards and transition support when a call-off or the framework ends.

What belongs in each call-off

The call-off defines one concrete piece of work and nothing more.

  • Objective and scope, with the Leistungsbeschreibung for this work package.
  • Acceptance criteria and the Abnahme procedure for deliverables, or reporting obligations for services.
  • Duration, team composition by role and start date.
  • Price for this call-off, derived from the framework pricing logic.
  • Client-side contributions: access, environments, named decision makers.

A call-off should be short. If it repeats framework terms or overrides them, the structure is drifting. A call-off template of no more than two pages, which engineering can complete itself and Einkauf only checks for price and volume, works well in practice.

Commercial levers

Minimum commitment versus flexibility

Providers price lower when they can plan capacity. A modest annual minimum, for example equivalent to one team for six months, can secure better rates without locking the company into more than it will use. Combine it with a clear rule on what happens if the minimum is not reached, such as a credit carried forward rather than a penalty.

Ramp-down notice

For finance, the most valuable term is often the notice period for reducing capacity. One to three months per team unit is common. Shorter is more flexible for the buyer but usually more expensive, because the provider carries the bench risk.

Price adjustment

Multi-year frameworks need a defined adjustment mechanism. Indexation clauses in Germany are subject to the Preisklauselgesetz, so the mechanism should be drafted with care. As a reference for negotiations, WTW expects median salary increases of 3.2 percent in Germany for 2027.

Blanket purchase order

On the finance side, a Rahmenbestellung, a blanket purchase order for the framework value or an annual budget, lets each call-off be booked against an existing PO. This avoids a new approval cycle for every work package while keeping spend visible.

The legal guardrails

A framework with call-offs is a common target for scrutiny when it is used to supply named individuals who then work under the client's direction. If call-offs read like requests for specific people, if the client assigns their daily tasks and integrates them into its own teams, the arrangement may be classified as Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 AÜG, with the consequences of §9 and §10 AÜG if no permit exists. Three habits keep the structure on the right side.

  1. Call off results or defined services, not people. Name roles and outcomes, and let the provider staff them.
  2. Keep direction with the provider. The provider's lead plans and assigns work within the team. The client steers through scope, priorities and acceptance.
  3. Document acceptance per call-off. Abnahme records under §640 BGB for Werkvertrag deliverables are evidence that the client bought a result.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. The structure of any specific framework should be reviewed by legal counsel familiar with AÜG and service contracting.

A sequence for introducing the framework

  1. Agree the framework with one provider for the first work package, so the first call-off tests the terms in practice.
  2. Run two or three call-offs before extending volume or term.
  3. Review the pricing logic and ramp rules after six months against actual usage.
  4. Add a second provider only if dual sourcing is a deliberate decision, not a reflex.

A framework only pays off when the provider can deliver the call-off structure it promises. niogod™ works with a framework model in which each call-off is a sprint-based Leistungsbeschreibung with Abnahme, delivered by a pod led by a German-speaking Projektleiter under a Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag. If your next engineering need is likely to be followed by another, setting up the framework first is usually the better sequence.
