Engineering Salary Budgets for 2027: What German Benchmarks Support

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Engineering salary budgets for 2027 are being set in a market that looks calmer than it has for years. Shortage figures have fallen, layoffs at large technology firms have released talent, and general salary increase plans in Germany are moderate. The risk for a scaleup is to read those averages as the right number for its own engineering team. This analysis sets out what the current benchmarks actually say, why the effective cost increase of an engineering team usually exceeds the headline merit budget, and how to build a 2027 figure that survives contact with retention reality.

The headline benchmarks

In its Salary Budget Planning survey, reported in September 2026 and based on 880 companies in Germany, WTW found a median actual salary increase of 3.1 percent for 2026 and a planned median of 3.2 percent for 2027. Life sciences companies plan slightly higher at 3.4 percent. For Switzerland, the same survey reported a 2026 increase of 2.4 percent. These figures cover all employee groups, not engineering specifically.

The labour market behind them has loosened. Bitkom reported in September 2026 that 79,000 IT positions were unfilled in Germany, down from 149,000 in 2023. Still, 76 percent of the companies Bitkom surveyed expect the IT skills shortage to worsen again, and 12 percent of companies with vacancies are looking specifically for AI specialists. The market is calmer, not relaxed, and that distinction matters most for senior and specialised roles.

The salary structure you are budgeting against

The Stepstone salary report 2026, based on more than 1.3 million salary records, puts the median gross annual salary of IT professionals in Germany at 66,750 EUR and of established software developers at 65,250 EUR. The progression by experience is steep: 70,500 EUR at 6 to 10 years, 77,750 EUR at 11 to 25 years. Company size has a large effect as well, with a median of 58,750 EUR in companies under 50 employees and 76,750 EUR in companies with more than 5,000.

For a scaleup, the last figure is the important one. Your senior engineers are benchmarked, by recruiters and by themselves, against large employers. Each year you grow, the comparison group shifts upward.

Why the team cost rises faster than the merit budget

Seniority drift

A team that hired mid-level engineers two years ago now has engineers who expect to be paid as seniors. Promotions are typically funded outside the merit budget, but they are a real cost increase. In a team of 20 with a normal progression rate, promotions alone can add one to two percentage points to the payroll increase.

Retention adjustments

Counter-offers and market corrections for engineers who receive external offers are rarely planned and almost always paid. They concentrate on exactly the people a scaleup can least afford to lose.

New-hire premium

Engineers hired in 2027 are hired at 2027 market rates, which can exceed the salaries of comparable existing staff. That creates compression, and compression creates pressure to lift existing salaries.

On-costs

Employer social security rises with salaries and, separately, with the contribution ceilings, which increase again on 1 January 2027. The pension contribution rate is projected to rise from 18.6 to 19.8 percent in 2028 according to the Rentenversicherungsbericht 2026.

A planning method

The following is an illustrative calculation for a team of 20 engineers with an average gross salary of 80,000 EUR, a payroll of 1.6 million EUR.

In this example, the effective increase is roughly double the headline merit budget. Employer contributions on that increase come on top.

Five rules for the 2027 budget

  1. Separate merit, promotion and retention. Three lines make the budget defensible and show the CFO where the money actually goes.
  2. Benchmark by role and seniority, not by company average. A general 3.2 percent figure says little about a senior ML engineer or an SAP architect.
  3. Hold a retention reserve and release it deliberately. Unused reserve returns to the budget. A missing reserve becomes an unplanned overspend in March.
  4. Plan Austria and Switzerland separately. Collective agreements in Austria and lower general increase rates in Switzerland change the arithmetic for DACH-wide teams.
  5. Decide which capacity should be fixed. Every permanent hire adds a cost line that grows every year. Capacity for time-limited work, such as a migration or a platform rebuild, does not need to compound.

Signals that your number is too low

Three signals in the current year indicate that a 2027 budget anchored on the general benchmark will not hold. First, more than one unplanned counter-offer in the last twelve months. Second, offers to new hires that exceeded the salary of comparable existing engineers. Third, recruiter outreach to your team that your engineers mention openly in one-to-ones. If two of the three apply, plan the retention and compression lines at the upper end of the range, and discuss with your CFO in advance which roadmap items move if the reserve is consumed.

The structural question behind the number

Salary increases are not a problem to be minimised. A team that is paid fairly stays, and stability is worth more than a percentage point. The structural question is how much of the roadmap should sit on a cost base that rises by 5 to 7 percent a year, and how much can sit on contracted capacity whose price is fixed for the contract term and adjusted only by agreed mechanisms.
