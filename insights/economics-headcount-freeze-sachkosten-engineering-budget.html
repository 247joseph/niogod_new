Headcount Freeze, Open Roadmap: Funding Engineering from Sachkosten

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Many Mittelstand companies enter 2027 with a hiring freeze or a tight cap on the Stellenplan, while the product roadmap still contains commitments to customers. Engineering leaders then face a familiar question: can the work be funded from the budget for external services (Fremdleistungen, part of Sachkosten) instead of personnel cost? It can, and in many companies it is the normal route. But it only holds up if it is presented openly to the CFO as a different commitment profile, and if the contract is a genuine service or work contract rather than disguised staffing. This article sets out how to make that case properly.

Why freezes are common right now

German industry is still cautious about headcount. The ifo Employment Barometer rose slightly to 95.5 points in October 2026 from 95.1 in September, but ifo noted that industrial companies still plan to cut staff, with the automotive industry expected to shed the most jobs. The IAB job vacancy survey counted about 1.15 million open positions in Germany in the first quarter of 2026, 105,800 fewer than in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Engineering skills remain scarce at the same time. Bitkom counted 79,000 open IT positions in 2026, and 78 percent of companies still observe a shortage of IT specialists. Demand for software keeps rising: Bitkom expects the German software market to grow 9.9 percent in 2026. The result in many companies is a freeze on new positions combined with a roadmap that needs more engineering capacity, not less.

How the two budget lines differ

AspectPersonnel cost (Stellenplan)External services (Sachkosten)
CommitmentOpen-ended, with notice periods and employment protectionLimited to the contract term and notice period
ApprovalHeadcount approval, often at board levelPurchase approval by value thresholds
Cost visibilitySalary plus employer contributions, rising each yearFixed fee per period, as agreed
Control of workEmployer directs the employeeClient defines results; contractor organises the work
Typical useCore capabilities, long-term ownershipDefined work packages, temporary or peak capacity

The difference in commitment is the honest argument. A CFO who freezes headcount usually wants to avoid long-term fixed cost under uncertainty. An external contract with a clear term and exit addresses exactly that concern, at a known cost, without creating a permanent position.

What makes the case credible

  1. Tie the spend to a roadmap item. Name the customer commitment or product milestone that the work delivers. A budget line for "extra capacity" invites rejection; a line for "delivery of module X by Q3" does not.
  2. Define the work as results. Write a Leistungsbeschreibung with deliverables and acceptance criteria. This is both good management and the basis of a Werkvertrag.
  3. Show the exit. State the term, the notice period and the cost of stopping. That is the figure that distinguishes this spend from a hire.
  4. Compare total cost honestly. Include your own coordination effort, not only the vendor fee. Present it next to the fully loaded cost of an equivalent hire.
  5. Agree a review point. Offer to revisit after two or three quarters: convert to headcount if the freeze ends and the work proves permanent, or stop if the milestone is reached.

The line you must not cross

Funding external work from Sachkosten is legitimate. Using a services contract to place people who work like employees is not. If external staff are integrated into your organisation and take instructions from your managers like employees, the arrangement may be classified as Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 AÜG, with the consequences of §9 and §10 AÜG, including the possibility that an employment relationship with your company is deemed to exist. For individual freelancers, Scheinselbständigkeit under §7 SGB IV is the corresponding risk.

Practical safeguards:

  • Work is assigned to the vendor as a package, not to named individuals as tasks.
  • The vendor's own lead, not your managers, organises and directs the vendor team.
  • Acceptance is based on results against criteria, not on hours present.
  • Vendor staff do not appear in your org chart, shift plans or HR systems.

Where a Betriebsrat exists, it has information rights regarding the deployment of external personnel under §80 Abs. 2 BetrVG. Involving it early avoids friction later.

Objections from Finance and how to answer them

"This is headcount through the back door." It is not, if the contract delivers defined results, the vendor directs its own team and the exit is short. Show the Leistungsbeschreibung and the notice period, and the difference becomes concrete.

"External capacity is more expensive per hour." Possibly, but the comparison that matters is total cost for the deliverable, including recruiting time, onboarding and the long-term commitment a hire creates. Under uncertainty, paying for flexibility can be the cheaper option.

"We lose the knowledge when the contract ends." Require documentation, code in your own repositories and a handover phase as contractual deliverables. Knowledge transfer then becomes part of the scope, not a hope.

A one-page template for the CFO

  • Roadmap item and business reason, in two sentences.
  • Scope as deliverables with acceptance criteria.
  • Monthly fee, term, notice period and exit cost.
  • Internal coordination effort in hours per week.
  • Contract model and compliance check (Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag, AÜG assessment).
  • Review date and the two possible outcomes: convert or stop.

The classification of a contract under the AÜG and social security law depends on how it is actually performed, not only on its wording. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice.
