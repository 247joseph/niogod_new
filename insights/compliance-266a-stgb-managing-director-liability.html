§266a StGB: Personal Liability of Managing Directors for Misclassified Staff

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When a contractor is reclassified as an employee, the company pays the contributions. The managing director may pay with something else. §266a StGB makes it a criminal offence for an employer to withhold employees' social security contributions, and the person who acts for the employer, typically the Geschäftsführer of a GmbH, is the one prosecuted and, under civil law, the one who can be held personally liable. For founders and CTOs who sign contractor agreements, the practical answer is a documented decision process: knowing which engagements carry status risk, deciding on each one in writing and escalating doubtful cases before they become years of arrears.

What §266a StGB actually says

Under §266a Abs. 1 StGB, whoever as an employer withholds the employee's contributions to social security from the collecting agency faces imprisonment of up to five years or a fine. Abs. 2 extends this to the employer's own contributions where the employer makes incorrect or incomplete statements or leaves the agency in ignorance. In particularly serious cases, Abs. 4 provides for imprisonment from six months to ten years. Through §14 StGB, the offence is attributed to the organ of a legal entity, which in a GmbH means its managing directors.

Two points matter for technology companies. First, the offence does not require that anyone was paid in cash or off the books. A freelancer who is legally an employee generates contribution duties whether or not anyone thought of him as an employee. Second, intent can be conditional (bedingter Vorsatz): it can be enough that a decision maker considered misclassification possible and accepted it.

Why the exposure lasts so long

Under §25 Abs. 1 SGB IV, contribution claims normally become time-barred four years after the end of the calendar year in which they fell due. For contributions withheld intentionally, the period is thirty years. In addition, §24 SGB IV imposes late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschläge) of one percent of the outstanding amount for each started month. Over several years, the surcharges can approach the size of the contributions themselves. DRV Westfalen, for example, reported for 2025 that its audits triggered by suspected undeclared work reclaimed about 16,1 million EUR in contributions and about 14,2 million EUR in late-payment surcharges.

Criminal limitation follows its own rules, and the Bundesgerichtshof clarified in 2020 (1 StR 58/19) that the limitation period begins for each contribution period separately. Even so, arrears running back five or six years are a realistic scenario in a long-running misclassified engagement.

Enforcement is getting more direct

Since 1 January 2026, the Finanzkontrolle Schwarzarbeit (FKS) can conduct investigations under §266a StGB on its own and apply for penalty orders, as summarised by Noerr after the reform entered into force. In 2025 the FKS initiated about 98.200 criminal proceedings, according to the customs annual figures reported by Handwerksblatt. Most of them concern sectors far from software, but the procedure is the same for every employer.

The status question itself is far from trivial. According to a Bundestag reply reported in August 2025, the Clearingstelle of the DRV decided 5.146 status cases as dependent employment and 7.706 as self-employment in the first half of 2025. Roughly four in ten cases that parties submitted voluntarily were classified as employment.

Civil liability: §823 Abs. 2 BGB

Because §266a StGB is a protective law, the social security carrier can claim the employee's share of withheld contributions from the managing director personally under §823 Abs. 2 BGB. This claim bypasses the limited liability of the GmbH. D&O insurance policies frequently exclude intentional breaches, so directors should not assume they are covered. The precise scope of cover is a question for each policy and should be reviewed with the broker.

A decision framework for managing directors

Personal liability turns on knowledge and intent. The most effective protection is therefore a process that shows that status risks were identified, assessed and decided in good faith. The following framework can be run in a quarterly management meeting.

Risk classTypical engagementDecision
LowCompany with its own staff delivering a defined result under a Werkvertrag, acceptance under §640 BGB, vendor steers its peopleDocument scope and acceptance, review annually
MediumService company delivering under a Dienstvertrag, some joint work with internal team, vendor lead presentCheck steering in practice, confirm that instructions run through the vendor lead
HighIndividual freelancer, long duration, tasks assigned by internal leads, paid by hoursConvert to employment, restructure, or file a Statusfeststellung under §7a SGB IV
CriticalFormer employee rehired as freelancer in the same role, or person integrated into HR systemsEscalate to counsel immediately, consider voluntary correction

Documents that demonstrate good faith

  • A contractor register with risk class, owner and review date for every engagement.
  • Minutes showing that the management discussed and decided status questions.
  • Written legal opinions or §7a decisions for cases classified as high risk.
  • Evidence that operational practice was checked, not just contracts (for example, how tickets are assigned).
  • Instructions to engineering leads on how to work with external teams without giving individual instructions.

None of these documents guarantees a favourable outcome. Together, they make it much harder to argue that a director knowingly accepted misclassification.

Where offshore teams fit

A managed team abroad does not remove §266a risk automatically. If individual engineers in India are in practice steered by your leads and billed by the hour, a German authority may analyse the relationship as personnel leasing, and §10 AÜG can then deem an employment relationship with the German client. The engagement belongs in the same register and the same risk classification. What lowers the risk is structure: a vendor entity that employs its engineers, a vendor lead who receives requirements and assigns tasks, a written scope and formal acceptance.
