Supplier Onboarding in Mittelstand Einkauf: An Engineering Vendor Pack

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

In many Mittelstand companies the decision to bring in an external engineering team is made by the CTO in a week, and then sits in Einkauf for two months. The delay is rarely about price. It is about the Lieferantenanlage: the supplier master record, the self-disclosure forms, the compliance attestations and the framework agreement that Einkauf needs before a purchase order can be raised. The answer is to treat supplier onboarding as a work package with its own document list and owner, started in parallel with technical evaluation rather than after it.

Why onboarding takes longer than it used to

Procurement departments have been given more to check. In its benchmark "Top-Kennzahlen im Einkauf 2026", published in November 2026, the BME reported that 89 percent of companies rank risk, resilience and supply security as their top procurement priority, while 76 percent score no more than half the available points on the study's transparency index. Put simply: Einkauf is asked to know more about suppliers than its systems currently show, so every new supplier is examined more closely.

Regulation adds to the list. Germany's NIS2 implementation law entered into force on 6 December 2025 and, according to an analysis by Reed Smith, widens the circle of supervised entities from about 4,500 to roughly 29,500. Entities in scope must manage cybersecurity risk, including risk arising from their suppliers. The Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz (LkSG) still requires companies with at least 1,000 employees to run risk analyses across their supply chain; the DIHK notes that the annual reporting duty is being abolished, but the core due diligence duties remain. A software vendor in India falls inside both lenses.

At the same time, the business case for external capacity has not disappeared. Bitkom counted 79,000 open IT positions in Germany in 2026, down from 149,000 in 2023, and 78 percent of companies still observe a shortage. The roles that remain open are the specialised ones, which is exactly where an external pod is usually requested.

A sequence that avoids the two-month wait

  1. Classify the purchase early. Agree with Einkauf in week one whether the engagement is a Werkvertrag, a Dienstvertrag or something else. The classification decides which reviewers are involved, and a late change restarts the cycle.
  2. Name one internal owner. Usually someone in the CTO office who chases reviewers. Without an owner, each department waits for the previous one.
  3. Run reviews in parallel. Data protection, security and tax reviews do not depend on each other. Send the full set at once rather than sequentially.
  4. Use a framework agreement. A Rahmenvertrag with call-off orders (Leistungsscheine) means the heavy review happens once. Subsequent sprints or pods are ordered against it without a new onboarding.
  5. Agree approval thresholds. Ask Finance which purchase order value requires which signature. Structuring the first order below a higher approval tier can save weeks without changing the substance.
  6. Set up the vendor record before the contract is final. Creating the master data, bank verification and invoicing channel in parallel removes the last delay between signature and first invoice.

What CTOs should check themselves

Einkauf checks the supplier. The CTO should check the deal. Three points deserve attention before handing over the file.

  • Scope written as outcomes. A Leistungsbeschreibung that lists deliverables and acceptance criteria supports a Werkvertrag. A document that lists people and hours reads as staff supply and may trigger AÜG questions from Legal.
  • Exit and transition terms. Procurement increasingly asks what happens if the vendor fails. A clear handover clause, code escrow or repository ownership in your own accounts answers that before it is asked.
  • Single point of contact. Reviewers want to know who on the vendor side is responsible. A named, German-speaking counterpart shortens every clarification loop.

Typical blockers and how to pre-empt them

The same objections appear in most onboarding processes. Security teams ask where source code and credentials live; the answer should be in your own repositories and identity provider, with vendor accounts that can be revoked in one step. Data protection asks whether personal data leaves the EU at all; often test data can be synthetic, which removes most of the transfer question. Finance asks about currency and payment terms; invoicing in EUR with a fixed monthly fee keeps the vendor record simple. Each of these is a design choice that can be made before the first meeting with Einkauf.

Note that NIS2, LkSG and DSGVO obligations depend on your company's size, sector and the specific data processed, and the reform of the LkSG is still in motion.
