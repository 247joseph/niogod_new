Staffing an Enterprise RAG Assistant: Team Shape, Data Access, Ownership

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Most German companies that start an internal knowledge assistant discover the same thing within a quarter: the language model is the smallest part of the work. The hard parts are getting documents out of SharePoint, DMS and SAP in a usable form, keeping the access rights of the source systems intact, and proving that answers are correct. That is a data engineering, security and evaluation problem, and it can be staffed with a remote team, provided the team is shaped around those three disciplines and the data access model is designed before the first index is built.

Why the demand has outrun internal capacity

Adoption has moved quickly. Bitkom reported in September 2026 that 57 percent of German companies with 20 or more employees now use AI, up from 36 percent in 2025 and 20 percent in 2024. The same survey shows where projects stall: 66 percent of AI users name data protection requirements as a hurdle, 50 percent describe data preparation as a major cost, and 51 percent cite infrastructure such as cloud and compute. None of these are model problems.

On the supply side, Bitkom's labour market study from September 2026 counts 79.000 missing IT specialists in Germany, fewer than three years ago, but 12 percent of companies with open roles are looking specifically for AI profiles and 33 percent say applicants lack knowledge of current technologies. A mid-sized company rarely finds a retrieval engineer, a data engineer and an evaluation specialist in the same hiring cycle.

The team shape that works

A retrieval assistant for a few thousand internal users needs four capabilities. They do not need to be four people, but each must have an owner.

CapabilityTypical workRemote suitability
Ingestion and data engineeringConnectors, parsing of PDF and Office files, chunking, metadata, incremental syncHigh, with pseudonymised or synthetic samples for development
Retrieval and applicationEmbedding choice, hybrid search, re-ranking, prompt templates, API and UIHigh
Evaluation and qualityGolden question sets, regression runs, hallucination and citation checksHigh for tooling, medium for building question sets (needs domain experts)
Security and permissionsMapping source ACLs into the index, tenant isolation, logging, deletionDesign remote, sign-off in-house

What stays in-house is narrower than most teams expect: a product owner who knows which questions matter, domain experts who write and judge the reference answers, and the data protection and information security functions who approve each new data source.

Design the data access tiers first

The single most important decision is which data the remote engineers can see, and when. A workable pattern uses three tiers.

  1. Tier 0, synthetic and public. Product manuals, published standards, synthetic documents that mirror the structure of real ones. All pipeline development and most retrieval tuning happens here.
  2. Tier 1, internal but non-personal. Technical documentation, process descriptions, internal wikis with personal data removed. Accessible to the remote team inside a controlled environment in the EU, with no local copies.
  3. Tier 2, personal or confidential. HR documents, customer correspondence, contracts. The remote team builds and tests connectors against Tier 0 replicas; production indexing runs in the client tenant under client credentials, and engineers see only logs and metrics.

Where personal data is processed on behalf of the company, a data processing agreement under Art. 28 DSGVO is required, and access from India is a third-country transfer under Art. 44 ff. DSGVO, typically covered by the Standardvertragsklauseln plus a transfer impact assessment. The tier model keeps the volume of such transfers small and auditable.

Permissions are a feature, not a filter

A common failure is to index everything into one vector store and filter answers afterwards. This leaks: a summary built from a restricted document can reveal its content even if the citation is hidden. The robust approach is document-level security trimming at retrieval time, using group memberships synchronised from the identity provider, and a test suite that queries as different personas and asserts that restricted content never appears. This test suite is an excellent first deliverable for an external team, because it is objective and can be accepted against clear criteria.

Evaluation as the contract currency

Retrieval systems drift whenever a source system, a model version or a chunking parameter changes. An evaluation harness turns that drift into numbers: retrieval recall on a golden set, citation correctness, refusal rate on out-of-scope questions, latency and cost per answer. For a Werkvertrag or a sprint-based Leistungsbeschreibung, these metrics are the natural acceptance criteria. A sprint that changes the chunking strategy is accepted when recall on the golden set does not fall below the agreed baseline and the permission suite passes.

Checklist before the first sprint

  • List the first three data sources and assign each to a tier.
  • Name the in-house owner who approves each new source.
  • Agree on the hosting region and the model endpoints, including fallbacks.
  • Write 50 to 100 reference questions with domain experts, including questions that must be refused.
  • Define deletion: how a document removed at source disappears from the index, and in what time.
  • Agree logging scope, retention and who may read prompts and answers.
  • Fix the acceptance metrics and baseline values in the Leistungsbeschreibung.

A realistic sequence

An illustrative plan for a mid-sized company: weeks 1 to 4 build ingestion for one Tier 0 and one Tier 1 source plus the evaluation harness; weeks 5 to 8 add permission trimming and the persona test suite; weeks 9 to 12 run a pilot with one department and tune retrieval against real questions. Tier 2 sources follow only after security has reviewed the logs of the pilot. This keeps the remote team productive from day one without waiting for approvals that typically take longer than the engineering itself.

The legal points above are general information and not legal advice; transfer mechanisms and processing agreements should be reviewed with your data protection officer or counsel.
