Statusfeststellungsverfahren under §7a SGB IV: A Guide for Tech Companies

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

German technology companies regularly engage freelance engineers, often through agencies or as part of mixed teams with external vendors. If the status of these freelancers is unclear, the Statusfeststellungsverfahren under §7a SGB IV is the formal route to a binding decision by the Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund. Used early and deliberately, it converts an open liability into a known position. Used late, it can confirm the problem it was meant to prevent. This guide explains how the procedure works since its reform in 2022, which parts are temporary, and how to prepare an application.

The procedure in numbers

The Statusfeststellungsverfahren is used at scale. Figures from the Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund, published in the federal government's answer to a parliamentary question in August 2025, show 23,052 procedures in 2024 and a further 13,212 in the first half of 2025. In that half-year, 5,146 decisions found dependent employment and 7,706 found self-employment; the remainder fell into neither category.

The figures also show how hard it is to change a decision afterwards. In 2024, 3,002 objections against status decisions were dealt with, of which 669 succeeded in full or in part. In the same year, 1,665 lawsuits were filed, and 455 court proceedings ended in favour of the plaintiffs. The lesson for tech companies is that the quality of the facts and documents submitted at the start largely determines the outcome. With Bitkom counting 109,000 unfilled IT positions in Germany in 2025, freelance engineers will remain part of many teams, and so will the need to classify them correctly.

What the procedure decides

Under §7a Abs. 1 SGB IV, the parties to a contractual relationship can apply in writing or electronically to the DRV Bund for a decision on whether the work is employment or self-employment. The DRV Bund unit handling these cases is commonly referred to as the Clearingstelle. Since the 2022 reform, the procedure determines the Erwerbsstatus, meaning employment or self-employment, rather than separately deciding insurance obligations in each branch. The decision is based on an overall assessment of all circumstances of the individual case (§7a Abs. 2 SGB IV), and other insurance carriers are bound by it.

An application is not possible if a collecting agency or another carrier has already opened a procedure to determine employment. Timing therefore matters: the procedure is a planning tool, not a defence once an audit has started.

Who should consider it

  • Companies engaging German-resident freelance engineers for longer periods or with high integration into product teams.
  • Companies receiving freelancers through agencies or intermediaries, where the freelancer actually works on the client's premises or systems.
  • Founders and managing directors with unclear status, where the collecting agency must apply in certain constellations named in §7a Abs. 1 SGB IV, for example for managing partners of a GmbH.

It is generally not the relevant tool for developers who live and work entirely abroad, because German compulsory insurance rules apply on a territorial basis under §3 SGB IV.

Features introduced in 2022

FeatureProvisionWhat it means in practice
Triangular relationships§7a Abs. 2 Satz 2 and 3 SGB IVIf a freelancer works for a third party and there are indications of integration and instructions there, the DRV Bund also decides whether employment exists with that third party. The end client can apply itself.
Decision before work starts§7a Abs. 4a SGB IVOn application, the decision can be made before the work begins, based on the contracts and the intended way of performing them. Changes within the first month must be reported.
Group opinion§7a Abs. 4b SGB IVAlongside an individual decision, the client can request an advisory opinion (gutachterliche Äußerung) covering identical contractual relationships based on uniform contracts.
Protection from a group opinion§7a Abs. 4c SGB IVIf the opinion found self-employment and a later procedure finds employment in an identical relationship, insurance obligation can start only with the later decision, for relationships concluded within two years of the opinion and subject to the conditions referred to there.
Oral hearing§7a Abs. 6 Satz 2 SGB IVIn an objection procedure, the parties can request an oral hearing after submitting the reasons for the objection.

§7a Abs. 7 SGB IV provides that Abs. 2 Satz 2 and 3, Abs. 4a to 4c and Abs. 6 Satz 2 expire at the end of 30 June 2027. The DRV Bund was required to report on its experience by 31 December 2025. Whether these features will be extended is a legislative question. Companies planning group opinions or pre-start decisions should take this date into account.

The one-month rule

§7a Abs. 5 SGB IV contains an important protection. If the application is filed within one month of the start of work and the DRV Bund finds employment, the employment is treated as starting only when the decision is announced, provided the person consents and has adequate protection against illness and for old age for the interim period that corresponds in kind to statutory health and pension insurance. The total social security contribution falls due only once the decision is final. Objections and lawsuits against decisions under Abs. 2 and Abs. 4a have suspensive effect (§7a Abs. 6 SGB IV).

In practice, this means: if you are in doubt, apply in the first month, not after a year.

Step sequence for an application

  1. Decide whether to apply before or after start. A pre-start decision under Abs. 4a gives early certainty but relies on the intended performance. A post-start application within one month preserves the Abs. 5 protection.
  2. Align with the freelancer. A joint application is common and avoids the hearing step in §7a Abs. 4 SGB IV where the decision matches the joint request.
  3. Prepare the documents. Contract, Leistungsbeschreibung, examples of deliverables and invoices, evidence of other clients, own equipment and business presence, and a description of how tasks are assigned and accepted.
  4. Describe the operating reality honestly. The DRV Bund evaluates actual performance. Descriptions that diverge from practice create a decision that will not hold.
  5. Answer requests within the deadline. Under §7a Abs. 3 SGB IV, the DRV Bund sets a reasonable deadline for missing information.
  6. Plan for the outcome. If employment is likely, prepare the alternative (employment contract, restructured engagement or a vendor-led model) before the decision arrives.

What the procedure cannot do

The procedure does not fix an operating model that integrates freelancers into the client's teams. It also does not answer AÜG questions directly: whether an intermediary supplies workers without a licence is assessed under the AÜG, although the factual analysis overlaps. For teams delivered by a vendor that employs its engineers and directs them itself, the status question shifts from the individual to the contract between the companies.
