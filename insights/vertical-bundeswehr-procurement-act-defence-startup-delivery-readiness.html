Bundeswehr Procurement Act 2026: Delivery Readiness for Defence Startups

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Germany's new Bundeswehr procurement acceleration act has changed the commercial terms for defence technology startups more than any funding programme. It allows advance payments to attract smaller bidders, introduces the innovation partnership for defence contracts and keeps the option to award large packages to general contractors without splitting them into lots. For a startup with 20 to 100 engineers, the consequence is practical: winning is now more realistic, but delivering on a phased development contract, often as a subcontractor to a prime, requires an engineering organisation that can plan, document and hand over work in contractually defined stages. The answer is to build that delivery readiness before the first tender, not after the award.

What the act changes

As summarised by CMS, the act entered into force on 14 February 2026 after the Bundestag vote on 15 January 2026 and applies until 31 December 2035. It replaces the procurement acceleration law in force since July 2022 and carries over many of its provisions. Three points matter most for startups:

  • Advance payments. Contracting authorities may grant advance payments where this increases the number of applicants or bidders, explicitly with start-ups and less capitalised firms in mind.
  • Innovation partnership. For the first time, the innovation partnership is available for defence and security contracts. It combines research, development and later procurement in one phased procedure.
  • No obligation to split into lots. Large defence contracts can be awarded as a whole, for example to a general contractor, without giving reasons. CMS expects this to put pressure on many mid-sized suppliers, which in practice means more startups will deliver as subcontractors to primes.

The money behind it

Demand is substantial. According to the Federal Ministry of Defence, the Bundestag's budget committee adopted the 2026 defence budget together with the Bundeswehr special fund with a combined volume of more than EUR 108 billion. Private capital is moving in parallel. KfW Research reported in October 2026 that German start-ups raised EUR 9.5 billion of venture capital between January and September 2026, more than in any full year since 2023, with AI-focused companies alone receiving EUR 6.3 billion. Germany now counts 43 unicorns, up from 12 at the start of 2021.

The constraint is people. Bitkom counted 79,000 unfilled IT positions in Germany in September 2026, and 97 percent of companies with IT vacancies reported difficulty filling them. A startup that wins a phased contract has to staff it against that market while continuing to develop its own product.

What a phased contract demands from engineering

An innovation partnership or a development contract with a prime is structured in phases with defined results, reviews and decisions to continue. Startups used to iterating freely often underestimate what this requires:

A readiness sequence before the first tender

  1. Separate product and contract work. Define which parts of the code base are background IP and which will be created under contract. Structure repositories accordingly.
  2. Introduce requirement traceability. A lightweight link from requirement to test is enough at the start, as long as it is maintained consistently.
  3. Define baselines. Every delivery gets a tagged, reproducible build, a change list and a test report.
  4. Prepare a work package template. Objective, inputs, results, acceptance criteria, risks, effort. The same template serves offers, internal planning and subcontracting.
  5. Plan capacity per phase. Estimate effort per phase and decide which packages your core team must own and which can be delivered by partners.
  6. Check security and export control early. Determine which artefacts may become classified and which technologies fall under dual-use controls, before any work is shared outside the company.

Working as a subcontractor to a prime

When contracts are not split into lots, many startups will deliver a subsystem to a general contractor. Primes expect the same discipline they apply internally: interface control documents, agreed test procedures and predictable delivery dates. Negotiate interfaces and acceptance procedures in writing, insist on access to integration environments early, and make sure that the scope of your contribution is described as a defined result. Advance payments, where granted, ease cash flow, but they do not replace a realistic plan for staffing each phase.

Pricing phases realistically

Phased contracts are usually priced per phase. Underestimating documentation, reviews and integration support is the most common reason a phase becomes unprofitable. A useful rule of thumb from general project practice is to estimate the pure development effort first and then add explicit line items for reviews, test reports, configuration management and customer coordination, rather than hiding them in a generic margin.

Checklist

  • Background IP and contract results separated in code and documentation
  • Requirement-to-test traceability in place
  • Reproducible baselines and delivery reports per milestone
  • Work package template used for offers and planning
  • Phase-by-phase capacity plan including partners
  • Security perimeter and export control assessment documented

This article is general information and not legal advice; the procurement rules for a specific tender should be confirmed with qualified counsel.
