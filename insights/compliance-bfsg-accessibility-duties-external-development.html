BFSG Accessibility Duties When an External Team Builds Your Digital Product

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Since 28 June 2025, the Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG) requires many products and services offered to consumers in Germany to be accessible. It implements the European Accessibility Act and covers, among others, e-commerce services, consumer banking services, e-books, telecommunications services and a range of consumer hardware such as computers, smartphones and self-service terminals. For a company whose web shop, banking app or device interface is built by an external engineering team, accessibility is now a property of the product that has to be specified, built, tested and accepted like any other requirement. Added at the end, it is expensive. Specified at the start, it is mostly a matter of discipline.

Who is affected and how much

The core facts are well established. According to guidance published by Grant Thornton in 2025, the law applies to newly provided services from 28 June 2025, fines can reach up to 100.000 EUR, and microenterprises with fewer than 10 employees and an annual turnover or balance sheet total of no more than 2 million EUR are exempt, but only for services. Product requirements apply regardless of company size. Certain transition periods run until 28 June 2030. The audience is substantial: the Federal Statistical Office counted about 7,8 million people with severe disabilities in Germany at the end of 2025, 9,4 percent of the population, and accessibility also benefits older users and anyone using a device in difficult conditions.

A B2B industrial software company may conclude that it is out of scope. Many deep tech companies have a consumer channel somewhere, however: an app that accompanies a device, an online shop for consumables, a patient portal. The scoping exercise should be done per product and documented.

The technical standard

In practice, compliance for websites and apps is assessed against the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which incorporates the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA for web content. For a development team, that translates into concrete criteria: text alternatives for non-text content, sufficient contrast, full keyboard operability, visible focus, correct semantic structure, accessible forms with clear error messages, support for screen readers and zoom, captions for media. Providers must also publish information on how the service meets the accessibility requirements.

Putting accessibility into the contract

  1. Name the standard. The Leistungsbeschreibung states EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.1 AA as an agreed quality under §633 BGB. Without it, accessibility gaps may not count as defects.
  2. Make it an acceptance criterion. Each user story with an interface carries accessibility criteria. An increment that fails automated checks or a defined manual test is not ready for Abnahme.
  3. Define the test method. Automated scans in the CI pipeline catch a part of the issues. Manual checks with keyboard and screen reader on the main user journeys are needed for the rest. The contract says which journeys and how often.
  4. Specify the design system. Accessible components in a shared design system prevent the same defect from appearing in every screen.
  5. Plan the documentation. The accessibility information for the service is a deliverable with an owner, not a text written by marketing after release.

Typical gaps in externally built products

  • Custom components (date pickers, dropdowns, modal dialogs) without keyboard support or proper roles.
  • Colour schemes chosen in design without contrast checks.
  • Error messages that rely only on colour.
  • Native app screens that ignore system font scaling.
  • Third-party widgets (chat, payment, cookie banners) that break the flow and were never tested.

Sequencing for an existing product

For a product already in the market, a full rebuild is rarely necessary. A sensible order is: audit the main consumer journeys (registration, search, checkout or core transaction, account management), fix blocking issues in shared components first, then address page-level issues, and only then work through edge cases. Each step can be packaged into sprints with defined acceptance criteria, which keeps the work measurable and contractually clear.

Who carries the obligation

The BFSG addresses the economic operator: the manufacturer, importer or distributor of a product, or the provider of a service. An external engineering team that builds the interface is none of these. The legal obligation therefore stays with you, and the only way to pass the technical work on is through the contract. That is why the standard, the test method and the acceptance criterion belong in the Leistungsbeschreibung rather than in a general quality clause. A vendor that is asked for "accessible" software without a reference standard has a defensible argument that it delivered what was agreed.

Enforcement in practice

The BFSG is enforced by market surveillance authorities, which can require corrective action and, as a last resort, restrict the offering. Fines are only one part of the picture. Commentators also expect accessibility gaps to be raised in competition law disputes and in procurement, particularly by public sector and larger corporate buyers who face their own accessibility obligations.

This article is general information and does not constitute legal advice; whether a specific product falls within the BFSG should be assessed with counsel.
