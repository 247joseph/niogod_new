Liability Caps in B2B Engineering Contracts: What §307 BGB Lets You Agree

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Almost every engineering services contract contains a limitation of liability. Most of them come from the vendor's template, which makes them Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen (AGB) under §305 BGB, and German law reviews AGB even between businesses. Under §307 BGB, a clause is invalid if it unreasonably disadvantages the other party, and German courts use the consumer catalogue in §309 BGB as an indication of what is unreasonable in B2B contracts too. The result surprises many buyers and vendors from abroad: a clause that caps "all liability" at the fees of the last three months can be invalid in its entirety, leaving unlimited statutory liability in its place. For a DACH company buying engineering capacity from India, knowing which caps hold is useful from both sides of the table.

Why the amount matters more than it used to

The damage profile of software work has changed. IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach study put the average cost of a data breach in Germany at 3,87 million EUR, after 4,9 million EUR a year earlier, and found that 16 percent of German breaches started through a supply chain or third-party system, at an average of 4,52 million EUR. In its 2025 Wirtschaftsschutz study, Bitkom estimated total damage to German companies from data theft, espionage and sabotage at 289,2 billion EUR, with 87 percent of companies affected. A cap set at a few months of fees can be a small fraction of a realistic loss in the scenarios that matter most.

What cannot be excluded or limited

Guarantees given§444 and §639 BGBA guarantee cannot be undermined by a cap on the same subject

Patterns that tend to hold

  1. Unlimited liability for the mandatory categories. Intent, gross negligence, injury to life, body or health, product liability and guarantees are carved out expressly.
  2. Limitation for simple negligence on essential obligations. Liability is limited to the damage typical and foreseeable at the time of contracting. A fixed amount can be stated as the parties' estimate of that damage, provided it is realistic.
  3. Exclusion for simple negligence on non-essential obligations. This is generally accepted in B2B.
  4. A separate, higher cap for data and confidentiality. Breaches of confidentiality and data protection obligations often carry the largest losses. A separate cap, aligned with the vendor's cyber insurance cover, is more defensible than one global cap.
  5. Annual rather than lifetime caps. An annual cap prevents the effective cap from shrinking to almost nothing late in a long contract.

Setting the amounts

A cap that is too low invites challenge under §307 BGB, and one that is uninsurable invites a vendor to accept terms it cannot honour. A workable method looks at three numbers: the annual contract value, the realistic loss in the main risk scenarios, and the vendor's verified insurance cover. Illustrative calculation: an engagement worth 600.000 EUR per year, with a realistic data incident scenario in the low millions, might combine a general cap of one to two times the annual fee for simple negligence on essential obligations with a separate data and confidentiality cap matched to cyber insurance of 2 to 5 million EUR. The figures are an example of the method, not a market standard.

Individually negotiated or standard terms?

Clauses that were individually negotiated under §305 Abs. 1 Satz 3 BGB escape AGB review. German courts set a high bar: the user must have genuinely put the clause up for negotiation and been prepared to change it. Redlines, email correspondence and alternative proposals are evidence of that. A vendor template accepted unchanged is almost always AGB. For buyers, this means a weak cap in a vendor's standard terms may not hold. For vendors, it means a cap they want to rely on should be negotiated visibly.

Checklist for your next contract review

  • Are intent, gross negligence, personal injury, product liability and guarantees carved out?
  • Is liability for essential obligations limited, not excluded?
  • Is there a separate cap for data protection and confidentiality?
  • Is the cap per contract year and linked to verified insurance?
  • Is there evidence of negotiation for any clause you rely on?
  • Are indirect damages and lost profits handled consistently with the carve-outs?

This article is general information and does not constitute legal advice; the validity of a specific limitation clause depends on its wording and the circumstances and should be reviewed with counsel.
