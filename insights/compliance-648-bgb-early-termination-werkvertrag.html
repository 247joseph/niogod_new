Ending a Software Werkvertrag Early: §648 BGB and What You Still Owe

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Roadmaps change. A funding round slips, a product line is stopped, a vendor underperforms. German contract law gives the client of a Werkvertrag an unusual right for these moments: under §648 BGB the Besteller may terminate the contract at any time until the work is complete, without giving a reason. The catch is the price. The contractor keeps its claim to the agreed remuneration, minus saved expenses and what it earns or maliciously fails to earn elsewhere. For software contracts, where much of the agreed fee is personnel cost that may or may not be redeployable, that calculation can be expensive and contentious. This guide explains the statutory model, the alternatives, and the clauses that make an early exit predictable.

The statutory model

§648 BGB contains three elements. First, the free termination right of the client until completion. Second, the contractor's claim to the agreed remuneration, reduced by saved expenses and by income from the alternative use of its capacity, including income it maliciously fails to earn. Third, a presumption: the contractor is presumed to be entitled to 5 percent of the remuneration attributable to the part of the work not yet performed. The contractor may prove more, the client may prove less.

In practice, disputes concentrate on the second element. A vendor that can show its engineers had no other project to move to will argue that it saved almost nothing. A client will argue that a vendor with a large bench should have redeployed them. Without a contractual rule, this becomes a matter of evidence after the relationship has already broken down.

The alternative: termination for cause under §648a BGB

Since the reform of German construction and work contract law that took effect on 1 January 2018, §648a BGB allows both parties to terminate for good cause without notice where continuation until completion cannot reasonably be expected. Typical grounds are serious and repeated breaches after warning, or a fundamental loss of trust. Where termination is for cause, the contractor can claim remuneration only for the part of the work performed. The client must usually set a deadline for remedy first. Termination for cause is a sharper tool, but it fails if the cause cannot be shown, and a failed termination for cause is often reinterpreted as a free termination under §648 with its remuneration consequences.

Dienstvertrag: a different logic

Many engineering engagements are structured as Dienstverträge under §611 BGB, where the vendor owes services rather than a result. Here §648 BGB does not apply. Termination follows the agreed notice periods or, absent them, §621 BGB. For services of a higher kind based on special trust, §627 BGB allows termination at any time, but only where there is no permanent service relationship with fixed remuneration. For a monthly fee engagement, the agreed notice period usually decides.

Why this matters more in 2026

The macro picture makes early exits more likely, on both sides. Creditreform counted 23.900 corporate insolvencies in Germany in 2025, an increase of 8,3 percent and the highest level in a decade. At the same time, Bitkom's September 2026 study put the number of unfilled IT positions at 79.000, down from 109.000 a year earlier. Companies are hiring again in some areas and cutting in others, and roadmaps are revised more often. An engineering contract that assumes it will run to completion is the exception, not the rule.

Clauses that make exit predictable

  1. Sprint-based scope. If the Leistungsbeschreibung defines work per sprint or per release, the "unperformed part" at termination is small and clearly bounded. A two-year fixed scope maximises the 5 percent base and the argument about it.
  2. Agreed termination compensation. The parties may agree a flat compensation for free termination, for example the fee for the current sprint plus a defined wind-down period. Courts review such clauses, particularly in standard terms, so the amount should be a realistic estimate of the vendor's loss, not a penalty.
  3. Wind-down obligations. Handover of code, documentation, credentials and a transition period at agreed rates, independent of the reason for termination.
  4. Partial acceptance. Acceptance under §640 BGB per increment means that completed work is accepted and paid. Only the open increment is in dispute.
  5. Clear ownership of work in progress. Rights of use in unfinished work results should pass on payment, so that a successor team can continue.

A short exit checklist

  • Identify the contract type actually lived, not only the label on the document.
  • Decide whether you terminate freely or for cause, and document the cause before you send anything.
  • Request handover of all work results and access within the contractual wind-down.
  • Calculate the expected remuneration claim on the statutory model before negotiating.
  • Confirm that no vendor personnel retain access after the effective date.

This article is general information and does not constitute legal or tax advice; the consequences of a specific termination should be assessed with counsel.

The cheapest exit is the one designed in at the start: short increments, partial acceptance and a defined wind-down. niogod™ pods work under a Werkvertrag with sprint-level Leistungsbeschreibung and Abnahme, which keeps the open part of the work small at any point in time. If a contract on your desk would be expensive to leave, it is worth restructuring before you need the exit.
