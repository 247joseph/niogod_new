Indian Engineering Wage Inflation and the Rupee: What DACH Buyers Price In

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

DACH companies that engage engineering teams in India often negotiate the first year's price carefully and leave the following years vague. That is a mistake, because two variables move every year: Indian salaries, which rise faster than German ones, and the euro to rupee exchange rate, which can swing by several percent within months. Neither should surprise a buyer. A well-built multi-year contract makes both explicit: who carries currency risk, how annual adjustments are calculated, and which caps apply. This protects the buyer's budget and the provider's ability to retain the engineers who carry the knowledge of your system.

The wage trend

Aon's annual salary survey for India, covering more than 1,200 organisations across 45 industries, reports an average salary increase of 9 percent in 2026 and projects 9.1 percent for 2027. Attrition rose to 18 percent in 2026, from 16.2 percent in 2025. The sector detail matters for engineering buyers:

  • Technology consulting and services: 6.6 percent in 2026, with 6.6 percent projected for 2027.
  • Technology platforms and products: 9.5 percent in 2026, 9.5 percent projected for 2027.
  • Global capability centres: 9.3 percent in 2026, 9.4 percent projected for 2027.

The spread is the key insight. Product companies and GCCs, which compete for the same senior engineers as any team working for a DACH deep tech client, are raising pay by around 9 to 10 percent a year, noticeably faster than traditional IT services. Nasscom data points the same way: GCCs added around 200,000 people in FY26, compared with about 110,000 added by IT services firms, and GCC salaries are reported to be up to 40 percent higher. Senior AI, ML and embedded profiles sit at the top of this range.

The currency factor

Exchange rates add a second layer. Based on historical data published by Pound Sterling Live, the euro stood at about 105.5 rupees on 1 January 2026, rose to a high of about 112.6 on 20 May 2026 and fell to about 101.6 by 20 November 2026. That is a swing of roughly 10 percent within one year. For a contract priced in euros with costs in rupees, a stronger euro widens the provider's margin and a weaker euro compresses it. For a contract priced in rupees, the buyer carries the swing directly.

Who carries this risk is a commercial choice, but it should be a conscious one. A provider squeezed by currency movements may respond by lowering seniority, delaying replacements or raising attrition, which costs the client more than the currency difference itself.

A fee frozen for three years against a cost base rising 19 percent is not a saving. It is a delayed problem that typically shows up as rotation of experienced engineers out of your account. A capped adjustment shares the burden predictably and keeps the team stable. Comparing this to German cost development is useful context: even with these increases, the absolute cost gap to German engineering employment remains substantial for most profiles.

Clause patterns for multi-year contracts

  1. Currency of the fee. A euro-denominated fee gives the buyer budget certainty. If the provider carries currency risk, a symmetrical review band (for example, renegotiation only if EUR/INR moves more than a defined percentage from a reference rate) keeps both sides protected from extreme moves.
  2. Annual adjustment mechanism. Tie adjustments to a published reference, such as a salary survey for the relevant sector, rather than to a provider's discretion. Define the reference, the date and the calculation.
  3. Cap and floor. A cap on annual increases protects the buyer's budget. A floor or minimum adjustment protects team stability in high-inflation years.
  4. Seniority and continuity commitments. Specify the seniority mix and named key roles, with notice and handover obligations for replacements. This prevents cost pressure being resolved by quietly downgrading the team.
  5. Productivity link. For outcome-based contracts, link price discussions to delivery metrics and accepted scope per sprint, not only to inputs.

The policy backdrop

The long-term framework is becoming more stable. India and the EU concluded their free trade agreement on 27 January 2026, with the EU opening 144 services subsectors including IT services, and commitments on business mobility for contractual service suppliers and intra-corporate transferees. Entry into force will take time, but it supports multi-year planning. Nasscom expects the Indian technology industry to reach about 315 billion USD in revenue in FY26, growth of 6.1 percent, which indicates that the talent base continues to grow despite rising wages.

Checklist for procurement and the CFO

  • Is the fee currency defined, and who carries exchange rate risk?
  • Is there a written annual adjustment formula with a named reference, cap and floor?
  • Are seniority mix and key roles part of the contract?
  • Does the budget for years two and three include a realistic adjustment rather than a flat line?
  • Is there a review date when both sides compare cost development and delivery?
