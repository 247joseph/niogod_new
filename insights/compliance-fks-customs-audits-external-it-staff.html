FKS Customs Audits of External IT Staff: What Changed in 2026

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Since 1 January 2026, the Finanzkontrolle Schwarzarbeit (FKS) of German customs works with a new legal toolkit: automated data analysis, its own investigative powers for contribution fraud and the right to inspect electronic records and cloud storage. For companies that rely on external engineers, this changes the nature of an audit. The question is no longer whether an inspector happens to find a contractor in your office, but whether your data trail, read by software, looks like hidden employment. The answer is a contractor file that is consistent before anyone asks for it.

What changed with the 2026 reform

The Gesetz zur Modernisierung und Digitalisierung der Schwarzarbeitsbekämpfung was passed by the Bundestag on 13 November 2025 and entered into force on 1 January 2026, as summarised by Noerr. Four elements matter for technology buyers:

  • Risk-based selection. The Generalzolldirektion becomes the central risk management office. Audits are planned with an operational information and data analysis system that checks stored data against risk indicators, and the authorities are expressly permitted to use artificial intelligence.
  • Own investigations. The FKS can now conduct criminal investigations for withheld contributions under §266a StGB and for social security fraud on its own and can apply for penalty orders. The public prosecutor can still take over at any time.
  • Digital inspection rights. Inspection rights now extend to electronic data and cloud storage, combined with automated analysis.
  • Machine-readable cooperation. Employers must cooperate not only in administrative audits but also in administrative offence and criminal proceedings, including the provision of digitised, machine-readable data. Failing to transmit such data correctly or on time is itself a fineable offence.

The sector list in §2a SchwarzArbG, which triggers duties such as carrying identity documents, was extended to hairdressing, cosmetics and platform-based delivery. Software development is not on that list. That does not place IT out of scope: the FKS audits every sector for compliance with social security and tax duties, and misclassified contractors are a classic finding.

The scale of enforcement

The 2025 enforcement figures, reported by Handwerksblatt from the customs annual balance, show the weight behind these powers. The FKS checked around 25.800 employers in 2025, up from about 25.300 in 2024, and screened nearly 300.000 people. It calculated total damage of about 675 million EUR, including unpaid social security contributions and taxes. Around 98.200 criminal proceedings were initiated, compared with 96.800 a year earlier, and the investigations led to prison sentences totalling just under 1.200 years.

These numbers are dominated by construction, logistics and hospitality. A technology company should not read them as a sign that it is out of focus. With automated risk analysis, selection is driven by data patterns, not by industry folklore, and the patterns that indicate disguised employment in software work are well known.

What an automated risk model is likely to see in IT

No public document describes the FKS risk indicators in detail, and it would be speculation to list them as fact. What can be said is which facts German courts and the Deutsche Rentenversicherung treat as indicators of dependent employment under §7 SGB IV and of Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under §1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 AÜG. Any risk model trained on audit outcomes will tend to reflect them:

  • Monthly invoices of near-identical amounts from one individual over years, without a defined deliverable.
  • Contractors with company email addresses, entries in the internal org chart or HR tools, and access badges.
  • Tasks assigned by your own team leads in your ticket system, with the contractor appearing in stand-ups like an employee.
  • Time sheets as the sole basis of billing, with no reference to a Leistungsbeschreibung or acceptance.
  • One contractor working exclusively for one client, using only client equipment.

Each of these can have an innocent explanation. Together, and without documentation that explains them, they describe exactly the profile a status or AÜG reviewer looks for.

Building the audit file before the audit

The practical response is to maintain, for every external engagement, a file that would explain itself to a reviewer who has never spoken to your team. The following structure works for Werkvertrag (§631 BGB) and Dienstvertrag (§611 BGB) engagements alike.

File elementWhat it should showTypical gap
Contract and LeistungsbeschreibungA defined result or service, owned by the vendor, with acceptance criteriaGeneric "support of the development team"
Vendor statusLegal entity, commercial register or foreign equivalent, own employeesSingle individuals invoicing as a company
Steering modelRequirements flow to the vendor's lead, who assigns work internallyClient leads assign tickets to individuals
Abnahme recordsSprint or milestone acceptance under §640 BGB, signedInvoices paid without any acceptance step
InvoicesLinked to deliverables or a fixed service feeHours times rate, no reference to output
Access and identityVendor accounts, labelled as external, time-limitedEmployee-style accounts and HR system entries

Because inspectors may now request machine-readable data, keep these records in a form that can be exported quickly: a contract register, an acceptance log and an access list are enough. A folder of PDFs that only one person understands is a liability under time pressure.

Day of the inspection: a short protocol

  1. Name one internal owner (usually HR or legal) who receives inspectors and coordinates requests. Inform your tax adviser or employment lawyer immediately.
  2. Cooperate fully with lawful requests. Under the new law, the duty to cooperate is broader, and obstruction creates its own exposure.
  3. Provide the contractor register and the relevant files, not ad hoc explanations from individual engineers.
  4. Log what was requested and handed over. If statements are taken, individuals should know that they may seek legal advice first.
  5. After the visit, review whether any engagement needs restructuring or a Statusfeststellungsverfahren under §7a SGB IV.

Why offshore delivery models need the same discipline

An engineering team that works from India is physically out of reach of a German inspector, but the contractual relationship and the payment trail are not. If an Indian vendor's engineers appear in your Jira as individual assignees, receive instructions from your product owners and bill by the hour, the arrangement can still be analysed as personnel leasing, with the consequences of §9 and §10 AÜG at the German end. The defensible model is a vendor-led team with its own lead, a written scope and documented acceptance, so that the facts and the contract say the same thing.
