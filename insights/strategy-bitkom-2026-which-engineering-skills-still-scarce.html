After the 2026 Cooldown: Which Engineering Skills Are Still Scarce in Germany

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

The German IT labour market has cooled faster than most hiring plans assumed. Bitkom's 2026 labour market study, published on 3 September 2026, counts 79,000 unfilled IT positions, down from 109,000 in 2025 and 149,000 in 2023. For a CTO, the risk is reading this as "hiring is easy again". It is not. The shortage has become selective: generalist roles are now fillable, while the specialist profiles that decide whether a Series A/B deep tech roadmap ships on time remain scarce. The right response is to split your hiring plan by scarcity tier, hire where the market has turned, and secure the scarce tiers through other channels before competitors notice the asymmetry.

What the 2026 data actually says

Three sources, read together, give a consistent picture.

  • Bitkom (2026): 79,000 open IT positions. Yet 97 percent of companies with IT vacancies still report difficulty filling them, and 76 percent expect the shortage to worsen again in the coming years. 31 percent receive practically no applications for open IT roles.
  • Bundesagentur für Arbeit (Engpassanalyse, published 2 September 2026): for 2025, the agency found no shortage in software development as an occupational group, while still counting 157 bottleneck occupations across the economy.
  • Deutscher Startup Monitor 2026: only 15 percent of founders name skilled labour as a major hurdle, down from 30 percent two years earlier, while 83 percent plan to hire in the next twelve months.

The aggregate is easing. The difficulty, however, has not disappeared. It has concentrated.

Where the scarcity has moved

Bitkom asked companies with vacancies which roles they are looking for. Software development and architecture account for 17 percent of searches, cloud and infrastructure for 14 percent, AI for 12 percent and IT security for 11 percent. The qualitative answers matter more: 33 percent say applicants lack knowledge of the latest technologies, 43 percent cite insufficient technical qualifications and 47 percent cite missing German skills.

Translated into hiring reality for a 20 to 100 engineer scaleup, this produces three tiers.

TierTypical profilesMarket state in 2026
1. FillableWeb and backend developers, generic DevOps, IT administration, junior QABroad applicant pools, falling time to hire
2. ContestedSenior cloud architects, security engineers, data engineers with production MLOpsApplicants exist, but strong candidates take multiple offers
3. ScarceApplied ML for perception or forecasting, embedded and real-time C++, robotics middleware, functional safety, regulated medtech software, SAP S/4HANA migration architectsThin pools, often requiring German plus domain knowledge

Tier 3 is where deep tech roadmaps get stuck. A medtech scaleup does not need "a developer". It needs someone who can write IEC 62304 lifecycle evidence. A robotics company needs ROS 2 and real-time experience. These are exactly the combinations the 33 percent "latest technologies" complaint describes.

Why the headline number misleads boards

Investors read headlines. When Bitkom reports that the shortage has halved, a board may push back on budget for external capacity or recruiting fees. Three counterpoints are worth preparing:

  1. The easing is cyclical. It reflects weaker demand in a soft economy, not a larger talent pool. Bitkom's own respondents expect the shortage to return.
  2. The composition shifted. Fewer open roles overall does not mean fewer open roles of the type your roadmap depends on.
  3. Demographics have not changed. The underlying retirement wave continues regardless of the business cycle, so any window in Tier 1 hiring is temporary.

A decision framework by tier

Use the cooling market deliberately rather than uniformly.

Tier 1: hire permanently now

This is the best window in years to build your permanent core team in fillable roles. Lock in hires where you have long-term need, and invest in onboarding so that the people you hire in a buyer's market stay when the market turns.

Tier 2: hire selectively, prepare a fallback

Run searches, but set a deadline. If a senior security or cloud role is not filled within a defined period, switch to a contractual delivery model for the specific work package rather than letting the role sit open.

Tier 3: secure capacity outside the local market

For scarce profiles, the realistic question is not "can we hire?" but "how long can we afford to wait?". Options include relocation hiring, international teams under a proper contractual structure, or a partner that already has the specialist combination. The decision should weigh time to productive output, not just cost per head.

Checklist: re-baselining your 2027 hiring plan

  • Tag every open and planned role as Tier 1, 2 or 3 using actual applicant data from the last two quarters, not assumptions.
  • For Tier 3 roles, record how many months each has been open and which roadmap milestone it blocks.
  • Separate roles that require German at C1 level from roles where English is sufficient. The 47 percent German skills hurdle often applies only to client-facing roles.
  • Decide which Tier 3 capabilities must be owned internally for IP or certification reasons, and which can be delivered as defined work packages.
  • Prepare the board narrative: easing headline, persistent specialist scarcity, and your mitigation per tier.
  • Review the plan quarterly. A cooling market can warm quickly once demand returns.

The practical consequence

A cooling market rewards companies that act asymmetrically: they hire aggressively where the pool has widened and refuse to wait passively where it has not. The worst outcome is a uniform hiring freeze justified by the headline, followed by a scramble for scarce specialists when the cycle turns and every competitor is searching at once.
