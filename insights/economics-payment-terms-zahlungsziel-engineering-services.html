Payment Terms for Engineering Services: Zahlungsziel and §271a BGB

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Payment terms are often treated as a finance detail that Einkauf sets by default: 60 or 90 days, sometimes with a long acceptance period in front. For an engineering services contract that logic backfires. Long terms are priced in by the vendor, they create disputes about what is due when, and under German law they are not unlimited. The better approach is to tie invoicing to sprint acceptance, agree a short and predictable payment term, and use the money saved on financing margin elsewhere.

What the market is doing

Payment terms in Germany are getting longer. Creditreform's analysis of the first half of 2026 found an average agreed payment term of 32.21 days, the highest value since 2019, with large companies at 35.51 days and small companies at 26.37 days. On top of that, invoices were paid on average 8.14 days late. The average receivable was outstanding for 40.35 days. For a small vendor, that is more than a month of salaries pre-financed for every client.

European rules will not change this soon. The European Commission proposed in 2023 to cap commercial payment terms at 30 days, but according to reporting by Global Trade Review the reform stalled after around half of the member states resisted it, and the Commission is expected to withdraw it. German law therefore remains the reference point.

The legal frame in Germany

  • §271a BGB, payment terms. Between businesses, a payment term of more than 60 days after receipt of the goods or services or of the invoice is only effective if expressly agreed and not grossly unfair to the creditor.
  • §271a Abs. 3 BGB, acceptance periods. Where payment depends on an acceptance or inspection procedure, a period of more than 30 days for that procedure is subject to the same test. Long acceptance windows cannot be used to extend the payment term silently.
  • §641 BGB, due date in a Werkvertrag. Payment for a Werk is due on Abnahme (§640 BGB). This is the natural hook for sprint-based billing.
  • §632a BGB, interim payments. The contractor may request Abschlagszahlungen for work performed, which supports monthly invoicing within a longer project.
  • §288 BGB, default interest. Between businesses, default interest is 9 percentage points above the base rate, plus a flat fee of EUR 40 per late payment. With the Bundesbank base rate at 1.52 percent from 1 July 2026, default interest is 10.52 percent per year.

Why long terms are expensive for the buyer

A vendor that waits 60 or 90 days for every invoice must finance its payroll in the meantime. That financing cost appears in the price, whether or not it is shown as a separate line. For a vendor in India there is an additional constraint: export proceeds must be realised within the period set by the Reserve Bank of India, which, according to Taxmann, was reduced from 15 to 9 months with effect from 5 June 2026. Disputed or very late invoices therefore become a regulatory problem for the vendor, not only a cash problem.

Illustrative calculation. Assume a fixed monthly fee of EUR 25,000 and a vendor whose cost of capital is 10 percent per year. Extending the payment term from 30 to 90 days means the vendor finances two extra months of fees, about EUR 50,000 on a rolling basis. At 10 percent, that costs about EUR 5,000 per year. A rational vendor builds that into the fee. The buyer gains two months of liquidity but pays for it at the vendor's financing rate, which is usually higher than the buyer's own.

A payment structure that fits sprint delivery

ElementRecommended patternReason
Billing triggerInvoice after sprint Abnahme or monthly against accepted incrementsPayment tracks delivered and accepted work
Acceptance window5 to 10 working days after sprint review, with deemed acceptance if no defects are reportedKeeps well within §271a Abs. 3 BGB and avoids open loops
Payment term14 to 30 days netPredictable for both sides, avoids risk premium
Disputed itemsPay undisputed part on time, escalate the rest within a fixed periodOne open defect does not block the whole invoice
CurrencyEUR, each side bears its own bank chargesNo short payments from FX or fees

Checklist for the contract

  1. Is the payment trigger tied to Abnahme or to accepted increments, and is the acceptance procedure defined with a deadline?
  2. Does the contract provide deemed acceptance if the buyer does not respond within the agreed window?
  3. Is the payment term stated in days net from invoice receipt, and is it within 60 days?
  4. Is there a rule for partially disputed invoices?
  5. Are default interest and reminder costs left to the statute, or replaced by a different agreed mechanism?
  6. Has Einkauf's standard term been checked against what the vendor actually quoted? A quote based on 30 days becomes a different price at 90 days.

What to tell Einkauf

Procurement teams often have a target for days payable outstanding, and an exception for one supplier needs a reason. The argument is not that the vendor deserves special treatment. It is that a short term is cheaper than a long one in this category, because the vendor's financing cost is priced in, and that tying payment to Abnahme gives the company more control than a long term does. Framed as cost and control, an exception is usually approved.

The application of §271a BGB depends on the overall circumstances and on whether terms are individually negotiated or standard business terms. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice.
