Making the Case for Remote Engineering Capacity to Your Betriebsrat

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

In companies with a works council, the most common reason an external engineering engagement stalls is not the budget or the vendor. It is that the Betriebsrat learns about it late, from rumours, and concludes that jobs are being moved abroad. Once that framing sets in, every subsequent discussion becomes defensive. The better approach is to treat the works council as a stakeholder with defined legal rights and legitimate concerns, to involve it early, and to present external capacity for what it usually is in a skills shortage: additional delivery capacity that protects internal roles rather than replacing them.

The numbers behind the conversation

Works councils remain a defining feature of German employment relations. According to the IAB Establishment Panel, as analysed by the Hans Böckler Foundation, 43 percent of employees in Germany worked in an establishment with a works council in 2024, and 37 percent in the private sector (establishments with at least five employees). For most mid-sized and large engineering organisations, the Betriebsrat is not an edge case but the normal counterpart.

The labour market supplies your argument, and the AI debate explains the anxiety on the other side of the table:

  • IW Köln estimated in 2024 that 16.5 million baby boomers will pass the statutory retirement age by 2036, while only about 12.5 million people of working age enter the labour market in the same period.
  • In Bitkom's 2026 IT labour market study, 18 percent of companies say they rely more on external IT specialists. External capacity is not an exotic step.
  • In Bitkom's 2026 AI survey, 57 percent of German companies with at least 20 employees use AI. 45 percent expect a slight decline in total headcount because of AI over the next two years, and a further 12 percent expect a significant decline.

Read together, these figures explain why a works council may hear "external capacity" as "replacement". Your case is stronger if you can show that the external work covers roles the market cannot supply and the demographic curve will not refill, rather than roles currently held by employees.

The legal ground you are standing on

The Betriebsverfassungsgesetz gives the works council several rights that become relevant when external engineers are engaged. The following overview is general and the precise scope depends on company size and the facts.

ProvisionWhat it coversPractical relevance
§80 Abs. 2 BetrVGTimely and comprehensive information, expressly including the use of persons who are not employees of the company, with time scope, place and tasks; contracts underlying their deployment must be made available on requestExpect to share the nature and scope of the engagement and the contract basis
§92 BetrVGInformation and consultation on personnel planning, including planned use of external personnelExternal capacity belongs in the workforce planning discussion
§92a BetrVGRight to propose measures to secure and promote employment, including alternatives to outsourcing; the employer must consider and respondPrepare a reasoned answer to the question: why not hire or train internally?
§87 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 BetrVGCo-determination on technical systems suitable for monitoring employee behaviour or performanceNew collaboration or tracking tools introduced for the joint work may require agreement
§99 BetrVGParticipation in hiring in companies with more than 20 employees; case law can treat integrated external personnel as hiringA clearly separated, vendor-steered team reduces this question; integrated individuals raise it
§106 and §111 BetrVGEconomic committee in companies with more than 100 permanent employees; Betriebsänderung where fundamental organisational changes occurRelevant if external delivery replaces existing internal functions rather than adding capacity

Note how the structure of the engagement interacts with these rights. A team that is integrated into your organisation and instructed by your managers raises §99 questions and, under AÜG §1, may also amount to Arbeitnehmerüberlassung, which brings further participation rights. A team that delivers defined outcomes under a Werkvertrag (BGB §631) and is steered by the vendor is a different situation, both legally and in how it is perceived.

What the works council is actually worried about

In practice, works council concerns about external engineering capacity cluster around five questions:

  1. Will internal roles be cut? The core concern. If the honest answer is no, say so in writing and explain how the external work relates to unfilled positions.
  2. Will internal staff be measured against the external team? Comparisons of output or cost between internal and external teams are a sensitive subject. Be explicit about how performance data is used.
  3. Will new tools monitor employees? Shared ticketing, time tracking or code analytics tools can trigger §87 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 BetrVG.
  4. Is personal data of employees leaving the EU? If the external team in a third country gets access to systems containing employee data, DSGVO Art. 44 ff. applies, and the works council will ask.
  5. Is this the first step of a larger relocation? Long-term intentions matter. Ambiguity is read as confirmation.

How to frame the case

The strongest framing in a skills shortage is capacity, not cost. Bitkom's 2026 labour market study reports 79,000 missing IT specialists in Germany and finds that 76 percent of companies expect the shortage to worsen. If your own vacancy data shows roles open for many months, that is the factual basis for the conversation. Present the external team as the way to deliver commitments the company has already made, while internal hiring continues.

Three elements make the framing credible:

  • Scope boundaries. Define which work packages the external team delivers, and which areas remain internal by design, such as architecture ownership or customer-facing roles.
  • Knowledge flow. Show how documentation and handover keep knowledge inside the company, so internal staff are not hollowed out.
  • Internal development. Where possible, pair the engagement with internal training or hiring. A §92a proposal from the works council can become a joint plan rather than an objection.

A step plan

  1. Before vendor selection: inform the works council chair informally that you are evaluating external capacity and why. Share vacancy data.
  2. During selection: prepare the information package expected under §80 Abs. 2 BetrVG: tasks, duration, locations, contract type.
  3. Before contract: agree how collaboration tools are used and whether a Betriebsvereinbarung or an addendum is needed. Involve the data protection officer on any transfer of personal data.
  4. At kickoff: introduce the vendor's lead to the works council if requested. A named counterpart reduces suspicion.
  5. Quarterly: report on scope, duration and internal hiring progress. Proactive reporting is cheaper than reactive justification.

Documents worth preparing in advance

  • A one-page summary of the engagement: purpose, scope, duration, team size, locations.
  • Vacancy data for the affected roles, including time open.
  • The contract type and the description of how work is steered.
  • A list of systems the external team will access and what data they contain.
  • A statement of intent on internal hiring and training.

What to avoid

  • Starting the engagement as a pilot without information, then formalising it once it works.
  • Arguing primarily on cost savings in a workforce that already feels pressure.
  • Placing external engineers directly in internal teams under internal managers, which complicates both the BetrVG and the AÜG position.

Keep the separation between internal staff and external delivery visible in documents, tooling and meeting structures, not only in the contract. This guide is general information, not legal advice; involve employment counsel for your specific situation.
