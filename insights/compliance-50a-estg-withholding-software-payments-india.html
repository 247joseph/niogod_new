§50a EStG Withholding Tax on Software Payments to Indian Vendors

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Paying an Indian engineering vendor raises two separate tax questions in Germany. The first, VAT under the reverse charge of §13b UStG, is well known to most finance teams. The second is less familiar: whether the German payer must withhold income tax under §50a EStG because the payment is a fee for the transfer of rights. If withholding was required and not made, the German company is liable for the tax. For most development and service contracts, the answer is that no withholding applies, but only if the contract grants the right kind of rights and the file shows why.

What §50a EStG covers

§50a Abs. 1 Nr. 3 EStG requires a German payer to withhold tax on remuneration paid to non-residents for the use of, or the right to use, rights such as copyrights. The rate is 15 percent plus solidarity surcharge, 15,825 percent in total, remitted to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt). Pure services, such as software development performed abroad, are generally not caught, because the non-resident has no German-source income from services rendered outside Germany.

Software contracts sit between these poles. A development contract usually ends with the transfer of rights in the code. Whether that transfer triggers withholding depends on what exactly is transferred.

The BMF guidance on software

The Federal Ministry of Finance clarified the position in its letter of 27 October 2017. As summarised by PwC in a November 2026 review of the topic, the key points are:

  • Intended use is not taxed. Where the customer only receives the rights needed for the intended use of software (installing, running, backing up), there is no limited tax liability of the foreign provider, so no withholding is needed. This applies to most licences and to SaaS used internally.
  • Exploitation rights can trigger withholding. Where the customer receives rights to edit, reproduce, distribute or sublicense the software for commercial exploitation, as in white-label, reseller or OEM models, withholding may apply.
  • Total buy-out is not a licence. A later BMF letter of 2 August 2022 excludes withholding where custom software is acquired in a full buy-out that amounts to an economic purchase: exclusive, unlimited in time and place, irrevocable, with nothing of value left to the developer.

For custom development, this means the drafting of the rights clause matters. A clause that grants the client exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable and unlimited rights in all work results, with no rights retained by the vendor beyond pre-existing tools, points towards a buy-out. A clause that grants a time-limited licence to reproduce and distribute the code, with the vendor retaining ownership, points towards a licence with possible withholding.

Treaties do not protect the payer on their own

The Germany-India double taxation agreement limits Indian-source withholding on royalties and fees for technical services to 10 percent under Article 12, according to Germany Trade and Invest. From the German side, a treaty can reduce or eliminate German withholding on payments to an Indian resident. However, PwC points out that the Bundesfinanzhof held on 13 October 2021 (I R 18/18) that the treaty alone does not protect the payer: to pay without withholding, a valid exemption certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung) from the BZSt under §50c EStG must generally be in place at the time of payment.

There is a de minimis rule. Where a treaty excludes German taxation, payments to the same foreign creditor of up to 10.000 EUR per calendar year can be made without a certificate. Since the Wachstumschancengesetz, for payments from 1 January 2024, crossing the threshold during the year only requires withholding on the payment that crosses it. A nil return may still be required.

The Indian side of the same payment

For completeness, the Indian vendor's taxation is its own matter. Germany Trade and Invest notes that India's domestic withholding on royalties is generally 20 percent under Section 115A of the Income Tax Act, reduced to 10 percent under the treaty with Form 10F and a tax residency certificate. In a 2021 decision, the Supreme Court of India held that payments for software resold or used by Indian customers are generally not royalties under the treaties. For a German client paying an Indian vendor for services, these rules mainly concern the vendor's own position.

Checklist before the first invoice

  1. Classify the contract using the table and record the reasoning.
  2. Review the IP clause together with your tax adviser, not only with legal.
  3. Where withholding cannot be excluded, ask the vendor to apply to the BZSt for an exemption certificate early, as processing can take time.
  4. Monitor the 10.000 EUR threshold per vendor and calendar year.
  5. Treat VAT separately: the reverse charge under §13b UStG applies independently of §50a.

This article provides general information on German withholding tax and is not tax or legal advice; contracts should be reviewed by a qualified tax adviser before the first payment.
