E-Invoicing from 2027: What It Means for Foreign Engineering Vendors

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

From 1 January 2027, German companies with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies, and from 2028 all companies must. Accounts payable teams are rebuilding their inbound processes around XRechnung and ZUGFeRD. That raises a practical question for anyone buying engineering services from abroad: does an Indian vendor have to send an e-invoice, and how should its invoices be handled? The short answer: the German mandate targets domestic transactions between businesses established in Germany, so a foreign vendor without a German establishment is generally outside it. What changes is your own process, and that is worth planning now.

The timeline

As summarised by Grant Thornton in 2026:

DateObligation
Since 1 January 2025All businesses must be able to receive e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; recipient consent is no longer required
Until 31 December 2026Transitional rules: paper and PDF invoices remain possible for issuing
From 1 January 2027Businesses with total turnover above EUR 800,000 in the previous year must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies
From 1 January 2028Transitional rules end; issuing e-invoices becomes the norm for all businesses

An e-invoice in this sense is a structured format compliant with EN 16931, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD in a compliant profile. A PDF sent by email is not an e-invoice but a "sonstige Rechnung". Small invoices up to EUR 250 (Kleinbetragsrechnungen) are excluded from the mandate.

Where foreign vendors stand

The BMF guidance of 15 October 2024, as summarised by Advant Beiten, applies the obligation where both the supplier and the recipient are established in Germany. A vendor in India without a German seat or establishment involved in the supply generally falls outside the issuing obligation. Its invoices may continue to arrive as PDF.

For engineering services from India, the place of supply is usually Germany and the German recipient owes the VAT under the reverse charge mechanism in §13b UStG. The vendor's invoice must then carry the required details, including the note "Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers", and must not show German VAT. That requirement is independent of the e-invoice mandate and remains essential for your input VAT position.

What changes in your own process

Once domestic suppliers move to structured invoices, the inbound flow in accounts payable becomes largely automated. Foreign PDF invoices then become the exception that needs manual handling. Speed matters: Creditreform measured an average receivable duration of 40.35 days in the first half of 2026, and manual exception handling is a common reason for slow payment. For a foreign vendor, a late payment can be more than an inconvenience; Indian exporters must realise proceeds within the period set by the Reserve Bank of India.

Three practical options exist for handling foreign invoices in an e-invoicing world:

  1. Accept PDF and capture manually or via OCR. Simple, but creates exceptions in an otherwise automated flow.
  2. Ask the vendor to send a structured format voluntarily. Many foreign vendors can produce XRechnung or ZUGFeRD on request. This is a contractual agreement, not a legal obligation.
  3. Reduce invoice volume. A fixed monthly fee means one invoice per month per vendor, rather than many time-and-material invoices with individual line items. That keeps manual handling small even if invoices arrive as PDF.

Checklist for contracts with foreign engineering vendors

  • Is the vendor's establishment status clear, and does any German entity of the vendor take part in the supply? If so, the mandate may apply after all.
  • Does the contract specify the required invoice content for reverse charge, including your VAT identification number and the reverse charge note?
  • Has the vendor agreed to a preferred invoice format, ideally structured, and a single delivery channel?
  • Do invoices reference the order number and the accepted sprint or period, so they can be matched automatically?
  • Is the invoicing currency EUR, avoiding exchange rate conversion in the booking?
  • Are invoice frequency and payment term defined, so the vendor record in your ERP can be configured once?

A short rollout plan for accounts payable

  1. Segment the supplier base. Split vendors into domestic suppliers inside the mandate, domestic small suppliers that may use transitional rules in 2027, and foreign vendors outside it.
  2. Define one exception path. Foreign PDF invoices should follow a single, documented route with a named owner, so they do not wait in a general queue.
  3. Update contract templates. Add invoice format, reference and delivery channel requirements to new contracts with foreign vendors.
  4. Test before January. Run a sample of foreign invoices through the new process in December, not in the first closing of 2027.

Recipient diligence from 2027

Grant Thornton points to a practical risk for recipients in 2027: it is not always visible whether a domestic supplier is below the EUR 800,000 threshold and may still issue a PDF. According to the BMF, an invoice in the wrong format should not be challenged on that ground alone if the recipient could reasonably assume the transitional rule applied, but the recipient is expected to exercise a prudent merchant's diligence. A simple step is to record, in the vendor master data, whether each supplier is domestic or foreign and which invoice format is expected. Foreign vendors are then clearly marked as outside the mandate, which avoids unnecessary queries in an audit.

The scope of the e-invoice mandate and the reverse charge rules depend on the specific facts, including any German establishment of the vendor. This article is general information and not legal or tax advice.
