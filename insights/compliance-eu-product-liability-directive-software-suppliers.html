New EU Product Liability for Software: Supplier Contracts Before December 2026

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

From 9 December 2026, software placed on the EU market is a product in the sense of strict product liability. Directive (EU) 2024/2853 replaces the 1985 regime, and Germany is transposing it through a new Produkthaftungsgesetz. For a DACH company whose firmware, app or AI component is written in part by an external engineering team, this shifts the question from "who wrote the bug" to "who placed the product on the market, and what can that party prove". The short answer: you remain the manufacturer towards injured persons, liability towards them cannot be contracted away, and your only lever is the supplier contract that governs recourse, evidence and updates.

What the new regime changes

The German government draft, adopted by the Federal Cabinet on 17 December 2025 according to a Greenberg Traurig analysis published in January 2026, follows the directive closely. Four changes matter most for software buyers.

  • Software is a product. Installed software, cloud-accessed functionality, embedded firmware and AI applications are covered. Open source is excluded only where it is developed or supplied outside a commercial activity.
  • The overall cap disappears. The draft removes the previous aggregate liability ceiling of 85 million EUR for serial damage and the 500 EUR deductible for property damage.
  • Evidence shifts. Where a claim is plausible, a court may order the defendant to disclose relevant technical information. Failure to disclose leads to a presumption of defectiveness. Defectiveness is also presumed where mandatory safety requirements were not met or an obvious malfunction occurred.
  • Control continues after sale. As long as a manufacturer can supply updates or upgrades, including through a third party, the product remains within its control. Claims are generally available for 10 years after placing on the market.

Only natural persons can claim: death, personal injury including medically recognised psychological harm, damage to private property and the destruction or corruption of data not used for professional purposes. A pure B2B platform therefore carries less direct exposure than a product that reaches consumers or patients, but most deep tech and industrial products reach people at some point in the chain.

Where the external team sits in the chain

The directive treats the manufacturer of a defective component as jointly liable where that component was integrated with its consent and caused the damage. Connected digital services can count as components. In a classic Werkvertrag arrangement, however, the external engineering team does not place anything on the market. It delivers work results to you, you integrate them, and you release the product under your name. Towards the injured person you are the manufacturer. Whether you can recover from the supplier depends on German contract law and on the recourse rules the transposition keeps.

This is not a theoretical concern for teams that are short of senior capacity. Bitkom counted 79.000 unfilled IT positions in Germany in its September 2026 labour market study, down from 109.000 in 2025, and the remaining gap is concentrated in specialised roles such as embedded, security and AI. The more of that specialised work moves to external teams, the more the supplier contract becomes part of your liability architecture.

A decision sequence for CTOs

  1. Classify the product. Does it reach natural persons directly or as part of another product? If yes, it is in scope from 9 December 2026 for units placed on the market after that date.
  2. Map the components. List which modules were written by external teams, which are third-party libraries and which are open source. Each needs an owner and an evidence trail.
  3. Close the evidence gap. Make sure the build history, test reports and design decisions for externally written modules sit in your repositories, not in a vendor's private tooling.
  4. Align the update promise. Decide how long you will support each release and make sure your supplier contracts and staffing plan can carry that promise.
  5. Re-paper recourse. Review liability caps and indemnities in existing engineering contracts. Many were drafted for a world in which software liability was mostly contractual.

What does not change

Contractual warranty under §634 BGB and fault-based liability under §823 BGB continue alongside the new regime. Acceptance under §640 BGB remains the moment at which the work result passes to you. The directive also does not turn an external Werkvertrag supplier into a manufacturer by itself. What changes is the burden on you as the party that releases the product: you need to be able to explain, with documents, what was built, how it was verified and how it is maintained.
