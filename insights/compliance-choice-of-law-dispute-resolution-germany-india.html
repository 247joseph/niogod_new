German Law, Indian Vendor: Choosing Courts or Arbitration That Can Enforce

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

Most German companies that buy engineering services from India sign contracts governed by German law with jurisdiction in a German city. It feels safe: familiar law, familiar courts, a German language procedure. The weakness appears only when something goes wrong and the vendor's assets, staff and bank accounts are in India. India has not declared Germany a reciprocating territory for the enforcement of court judgments, so a German judgment cannot simply be executed there. A foreign arbitral award from a German seat, by contrast, is enforceable under India's implementation of the New York Convention. This analysis explains the consequences and sets out a dispute clause architecture that works in both directions.

The commercial context

German-Indian business is growing. Germany Trade and Invest calculated bilateral trade at a record 31,3 billion EUR in 2025, up 1,5 percent on the previous year. NASSCOM projected revenues of the Indian technology sector at 282,6 billion USD for the 2025 financial year, with a workforce of about 5,8 million. A growing share of that work is delivered to Mittelstand and scaleup clients in DACH who rarely litigate abroad and are therefore unfamiliar with the enforcement map. Disputes remain rare, but contracts are drafted for the rare case.

Step one: governing law

Under Article 3 of the Rome I Regulation, which German courts apply, the parties may choose the governing law freely. Without a choice, Article 4 Rome I points to the law of the country where the service provider has its habitual residence. For a contract with an Indian vendor, that means Indian law applies by default unless the contract says otherwise. German law is a reasonable choice for a German client, particularly where the contract relies on German concepts such as Werkvertrag, Abnahme under §640 BGB and the distinction from Arbeitnehmerüberlassung. Indian courts generally respect an express choice of foreign law in commercial contracts, subject to Indian public policy and mandatory rules.

India ratified the New York Convention on 13 July 1960 and applies it only to awards made in countries notified by the Indian government in the Official Gazette. The Federal Republic of Germany is among the notified countries. The list of reciprocating territories for court judgments, by contrast, includes the United Kingdom, Singapore, the United Arab Emirates, Hong Kong, New Zealand, Bangladesh and a small number of others, but not Germany.

A clause architecture that works

  1. Governing law: German law, excluding the UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods where sale elements are present.
  2. Escalation ladder: operational escalation to the project leads, then to management, each with a fixed period of for example 15 business days, before formal proceedings.
  3. Arbitration: institutional arbitration, for example under the DIS or ICC rules, seat in a German city, one arbitrator for disputes below an agreed amount and three above, language English or German.
  4. Interim relief: an express statement that the parties may seek interim measures from state courts. Since the 2015 amendment, section 9 of the Indian Act can be available for foreign-seated arbitrations unless the parties exclude it, which matters for urgent cases such as data or code handover.
  5. Carve-out for payment claims: optionally, undisputed invoices may be pursued in state court at the claimant's choice.
  6. Service of process: named addresses and email addresses for notices in both countries.

What to check in existing contracts

  • Does the contract choose a governing law at all, or will Article 4 Rome I point to Indian law?
  • If German courts are chosen, does the vendor have assets in Germany or the EU?
  • If arbitration is chosen, is the seat in a New York Convention country notified by India?
  • Is the clause consistent across master agreement, statements of work and data processing agreement?
  • Are code escrow and handover duties enforceable quickly, independent of the main dispute?

A dispute clause also shapes behaviour long before any dispute. A vendor that knows an award can be enforced against it in India negotiates differently from one that knows a German judgment will be hard to execute. The clause is therefore part of governance, not only a legal formality.

This article is general information and does not constitute legal advice; dispute clauses for a specific contract should be drafted with counsel qualified in German and Indian law.

The most practical protection is a delivery structure in which disputes rarely escalate: code in the client's repositories, increments accepted sprint by sprint, and a German-speaking counterpart who resolves issues early. That is the structure niogod™ pods use, with a Projektleiter as the first point of escalation and a Werkvertrag with clear Abnahme behind it. If your current contracts point to German courts only, it is worth asking where a judgment would actually be enforced.
