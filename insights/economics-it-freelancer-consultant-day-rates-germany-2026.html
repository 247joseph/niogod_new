IT Freelancer and Consultant Day Rates in Germany 2026: A Benchmark

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

External rates are the reference point for almost every engineering capacity decision in Germany. A CFO comparing a permanent hire with external support needs a defensible number for what external capacity costs, and Einkauf needs a benchmark to judge whether a quote is reasonable. In 2026 two published data sets give a clear picture: rates for independent IT freelancers have levelled off, and day rates for consultants from IT service firms declined at the top end for the first time in years. This analysis summarises both, explains why they are not directly comparable, and shows how to turn them into a decision.

Freelancer rates: stable at a high level

The Freelancer-Kompass 2026, published by the platform freelancermap in July 2026, reports a median hourly rate of 95 EUR for IT freelancers in the DACH region, with a mean of 97 EUR. The spread by specialisation is significant.

SpecialisationMedian hourly rate 2026
SAP and ERP120 EUR
Data and analytics100 EUR
IT infrastructure95 EUR
Software and web development90 EUR

At eight hours, a median software developer rate of 90 EUR corresponds to a day rate of 720 EUR, and the SAP median to 960 EUR. Note that the 2026 report uses medians, while some earlier editions headlined averages, so year-on-year comparisons across editions should be made with care.

Consultant day rates: the end of the rally

For staff from IT service providers, CIO magazine published benchmark data from the firm Metrics in March 2026. The figures are average European day rates for an eight-hour day, excluding VAT and travel, based on contracts signed in the preceding 12 to 18 months.

Role20252026
Developer, more than 3 years of experience793 EUR805 EUR
Senior developer, more than 7 years1,162 EUR1,192 EUR
Solution design and delivery lead, more than 5 years1,571 EUR1,498 EUR

Developer rates still rose slightly, while the top tier fell by 4.6 percent. In infrastructure, the top tier for project management and architecture fell by 7.2 percent. The pattern fits the broader market: Lünendonk reported in June 2026 that the German IT services market grew organically by only 2.9 percent in 2025 and that employment at IT service providers declined by 0.6 percent, the first decline in more than twenty years.

Why the two data sets differ

A freelancer at 720 EUR per day and a consultancy developer at 805 EUR per day are not the same product at different prices. The freelancer rate covers one person, with no substitute if that person is ill or leaves, and with the client carrying the coordination effort. The consultancy rate includes the provider's overhead, account management, replacement obligations and, depending on the contract, delivery responsibility. The gap between the two is the price of those elements.

A second difference is legal. Long-running engagements of individual freelancers who work like team members carry the risk of Scheinselbständigkeit under §7 SGB IV. In the Bitkom IT labour market study of September 2026, 49 percent of companies called for a reform of the Statusfeststellungsverfahren, which shows how present the issue is in practice. A provider that delivers through its own employees under a Werkvertrag or Dienstvertrag shifts that specific risk, though it raises the separate question of Arbeitnehmerüberlassung if the client directs the work.

Turning rates into a make-or-buy number

The useful comparison is not rate against salary, but cost per productive day. The following is an illustrative calculation.

OptionAssumptionAnnual cost for about 200 productive days
Permanent senior engineer85,000 EUR salary, 21 percent employer on-costs, 8,000 EUR equipment, tools and overhead110,850 EUR, or about 554 EUR per productive day
Freelancer, software development720 EUR per day, 200 billed days144,000 EUR
Consultancy senior developer1,192 EUR per day, 200 billed days238,400 EUR

On this basis permanent employment is the cheapest per productive day, before acquisition cost, ramp-up and the fixed nature of the commitment are considered. Those factors change the picture for time-limited work. For a six-month need, the permanent option carries recruiting cost, a notice period and the question of what happens after the project, while the freelancer and the consultancy can be ended with the engagement.

Using the benchmark in procurement

  • Ask for rates on a comparable basis. Eight-hour day, excluding VAT and travel, with the included services listed. Hourly and daily quotes otherwise mix.
  • Check the seniority mapping. A senior rate for a profile with four years of experience is a hidden price increase.
  • Price the substitution guarantee. If the provider must replace a person within a defined period at its own cost, that is worth a premium over a freelancer rate.
  • Watch the blended rate in team offers. Teams with a lead, seniors and juniors should be compared on blended cost per sprint, not on the cheapest line.
  • Use the 2026 softening. With top-tier rates declining, multi-year rate cards signed at 2024 levels deserve a review at renewal.

What the benchmark does not show

Published rates describe what was agreed, not what was delivered. Two engagements at the same day rate can differ widely in output, depending on how clearly the work was specified, how quickly decisions were made on the client side and how much context the external person had to build first. Utilisation also matters. A freelancer billed for 200 days but blocked by missing access for the first two weeks has cost ten unproductive days. For a fair comparison, track cost per accepted work package over the first two or three months of any engagement, and use the published rates only as the entry point to that discussion.

Where offshore capacity fits

Both data sets describe European rates. Teams delivered from India price below them, but the comparison only holds when delivery responsibility, communication overhead and legal structure are equivalent. A quote is only cheaper if the same result is accepted at the end of the sprint. This is general information, not legal advice on the classification of any specific engagement.
