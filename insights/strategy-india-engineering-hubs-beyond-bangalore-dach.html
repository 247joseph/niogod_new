India's Engineering Hubs Beyond Bangalore: Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kerala

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When DACH companies say India, they usually mean Bangalore. That is a reasonable starting point, but it is an incomplete map. India's engineering capacity is spread across several metropolitan clusters, each with its own industrial history, employer mix and talent profile. For a CTO evaluating a delivery partner, the city where the team actually sits says something about the experience the engineers are likely to bring, how competitive local hiring is, and how stable the team will be. This guide profiles the main hubs and gives a method for matching them to your workload.

The scale behind the clusters

According to a 2025 estimate by the real estate consultancy Vestian, India hosts roughly 1,700 global capability centres (GCCs), the in-house engineering and operations centres of multinational companies. Bengaluru accounts for 487 of them, about 29 percent. Hyderabad follows with 273, Pune with 178 and Chennai with 162. Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai together hold around 55 percent of the total. GCC counts are a proxy, not a full picture, but they indicate where multinational engineering work, and therefore experienced engineers, are concentrated.

More recent industry data points in the same direction. The Nasscom and Zinnov report published in May 2026 counts 2,117 GCCs in India employing 2.36 million professionals, with revenue of USD 98.4 billion in FY26 (the methodology differs from Vestian's, so the two counts are not directly comparable). Nasscom's February 2026 strategic review puts the technology industry's direct workforce at 5.95 million in FY26, with 135,000 net additions and more than 2 million employees upskilled in AI. A Zinnov study with the Karnataka Digital Economy Mission found that Bengaluru alone hosted more than 230 mid-market GCCs as of March 2025, about half of India's total in that segment, employing around 74,000 people.

For a DACH buyer the implication is twofold: the overall pool is large, and the strongest employers concentrate in a few cities, which shapes the competition for experienced engineers in each hub.

City profiles

Bengaluru

The deepest and most diverse market: product companies, research centres of global technology firms, a large startup ecosystem and many GCCs in semiconductors, automotive software, AI and enterprise platforms. The upside is breadth of experience. The downside is competition. Engineers with sought-after skills receive frequent offers, so retention depends on the quality of the work and of the employer, not only on pay.

Pune

Pune grew up around manufacturing and the automotive industry, and several German and European OEMs and suppliers operate plants and engineering centres in the region. That history produces engineers comfortable with industrial software, embedded systems, PLM and manufacturing IT. For a Mittelstand machine builder or automotive supplier, Pune often offers a shorter vocabulary gap than a pure internet-product market.

Hyderabad

Hyderabad has become a major location for large technology and pharmaceutical multinationals, with strong representation in cloud platforms, data engineering and life sciences IT. For medtech and pharma-adjacent software, where validated processes and documentation discipline matter, the local employer mix is relevant.

Chennai

Chennai combines a long tradition of IT services with an automotive and electronics manufacturing base. It is known for SaaS companies and for engineering services in automotive and industrial domains. It is often perceived as a market with somewhat lower attrition than Bengaluru, although this varies by employer and should be checked rather than assumed.

Kerala: Thiruvananthapuram, Kochi, Kozhikode

Kerala hosts state-backed technology parks in all three cities, including Technopark in Thiruvananthapuram, one of India's earliest technology parks, and Infopark in Kochi. The state consistently ranks at the top of Indian states on literacy and education indicators, and many engineers from Kerala work in Bengaluru, Chennai or abroad. For delivery partners, Kerala's appeal is a combination of strong graduates, lower cost of living than the largest metros and engineers who prefer to stay close to home, which can support retention. The trade-off is a smaller pool of senior specialists in niche domains, so senior roles are often anchored in a larger metro.

How to evaluate a hub claim from a vendor

  1. Ask where each named engineer sits. Distributed teams across cities are normal, but you should know the actual locations and how the team meets.
  2. Ask about local competition. Which employers does the vendor lose people to in that city? A vendor who cannot answer has not thought about retention.
  3. Check domain experience, not city reputation. Request anonymised project descriptions in your domain. A city known for automotive does not mean the team has automotive experience.
  4. Check senior coverage. In smaller clusters, ask where the architectural lead sits and how they are involved.
  5. Check continuity mechanisms. Documentation standards, overlap periods when a team member changes, and shadowing practices matter more than headline attrition claims.
  6. Check German-language interface. Some cities have Goethe-Institut (Max Mueller Bhavan) or Goethe-Zentrum presences and local German learners, but fluent client-facing German is rare everywhere. Ask who speaks to your team.

Infrastructure and practical points

All Indian hubs share one time zone, IST (UTC+5:30), so city choice does not affect overlap with DACH working hours. Commercial office infrastructure, power backup and connectivity in established technology parks are generally reliable; home-office setups should be covered by the vendor's security policy, including device management and network requirements. The data protection framework is national: DSGVO Art. 44 ff. transfers from the EU to India require Standardvertragsklauseln and a transfer impact assessment, independent of the city.

Single-site or multi-site

Many vendors operate a main site with additional engineers in other cities. That is not a weakness if three conditions hold: architecture and quality ownership sits clearly with one person, team members meet in person at regular intervals, and security policies apply equally at every work location. Ask how often the team meets physically, who owns code review, and how access to your systems is controlled across locations. A multi-site setup without that clarity recreates the coordination problems you hired a delivery partner to avoid.

The takeaway

Bengaluru remains the reference market. The broader point is that India is not one market. Treat the city as one input into a vendor assessment, alongside leadership, domain experience and contract structure.
