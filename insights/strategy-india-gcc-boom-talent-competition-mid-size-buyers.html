India's GCC Boom: How Mid-Size DACH Companies Compete for Engineers

JJ
Joseph Jose
Founder & CEO, niogod™

When German, Austrian and Swiss companies think about engineering in India, many still picture the classic IT services model: large vendors, large teams, cost arbitrage. The market has moved. The fastest-growing employers of senior engineering talent in India are now global capability centres, the captive engineering and technology units that multinational companies run for themselves. For a DACH scaleup with 40 engineers or a Mittelstand firm with a small software team, this changes the competitive landscape. You are no longer only competing with Indian service firms for talent, but with the in-house centres of the largest corporations in the world. The answer is not to copy their model, but to choose an access route that fits your scale.

The size of India's engineering base

Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 estimates that India's technology industry generated revenue of USD 315 billion in fiscal year 2026, growth of 6,1 percent, with exports of USD 246 billion. Engineering research and development services alone account for USD 63 billion. The industry employed about 5,95 million people in fiscal 2026, up 2,3 percent from 5,82 million a year earlier. Nasscom also reports that more than 2 million professionals were upskilled in AI during the year, including 200.000 to 300.000 trained in advanced AI capabilities.

Growth in headcount has slowed compared with the early 2020s, partly because AI is changing how service work is staffed. But the base is large, and its senior layer in particular is deeper than in any European market.

The rise of global capability centres

The joint Nasscom and Zinnov report published in 2026 counts 2.117 global capability centres in India, generating USD 98,4 billion in revenue in fiscal 2026 and employing about 2,36 million professionals, including around 250.000 AI specialists. The number of centres has grown 32 percent since fiscal 2021.

These centres no longer run only back-office or testing work. Many now own product engineering, platform development, data and AI programmes for their parent companies. They recruit aggressively at senior level, offer the brand of a global company, and often pay above the local services market. For experienced engineers in Bangalore, Hyderabad or Pune, a GCC role is frequently the first choice.

What this means for a mid-size DACH buyer

A company with 20 to 100 engineers in total cannot realistically build its own capability centre in India. A GCC needs a minimum scale to justify a local entity, leadership, HR, facilities, compliance and retention programmes. Below roughly a few dozen engineers in one location, the overhead per engineer becomes disproportionate, and the centre struggles to offer the career paths that keep senior people.

At the same time, the mid-size buyer faces the GCCs in the talent market whenever it tries to recruit directly, hire freelancers or work with a small vendor whose engineers are constantly approached by larger employers. The result is high attrition in exactly the senior roles that hold system knowledge.

How to compete without a GCC

  1. Compete on substance, not salary. Senior engineers in India choose GCCs partly for the quality of the problems. Deep tech work in robotics, industrial software, medtech or AI is attractive. Make the technical challenge visible in how you describe the work.
  2. Offer direct contact with the product. Engineers who speak with the people who use their software stay longer than engineers who only receive tickets. A structured weekly exchange with your product owner is worth more than a perk.
  3. Insist on continuity in the contract. If you work with a provider, define named key roles, notice periods for replacements and handover obligations. Ask how the provider retains senior people against GCC offers.
  4. Keep the unit small and senior. A small team with a strong senior layer survives individual departures better than a large junior-heavy team, because knowledge is documented and shared rather than concentrated.
  5. Measure retention as a delivery metric. Track tenure of key people on your account alongside velocity and quality. Rising turnover is an early warning for knowledge loss.

Questions to ask any India partner

Before committing to a provider, ask for specifics that reveal how the GCC boom affects them. What was annual attrition among senior engineers over the last two years? How long does it take to replace a departing key person, and who pays for the overlap? Which roles in the proposed team are named, and what is the notice period for changing them? How is knowledge documented so that a departure does not stop delivery? Clear answers to these questions matter more than the day rate, because the cost of losing a senior engineer mid-project is usually several months of reduced output.

Reading the trend for 2027

The GCC model will keep expanding as multinational companies move more product and AI work to India. That raises the floor for senior engineering compensation and makes loosely structured sourcing models riskier. It also deepens the pool of engineers who have worked on global products to European quality standards. Mid-size DACH companies that secure stable, well-led access to a small part of that pool will benefit from the same talent base that the largest corporations are investing in.
